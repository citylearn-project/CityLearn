    KPIs

    KPIs measure the completed simulation: energy, cost, emissions, service, comfort and electrical performance. They are available from Python and CSV exports; using the UI is optional. This page is the entry point to every KPI page.

    Choose what to read

    • How to read the scorecard — start here: how to read a compact set of KPIs and compare controllers.

    • Default scorecard KPI names — the default selection, returned by env.scorecard().

    • KPIs Reference — what each family measures, its equations, district and portfolio aggregation, and undefined values.

    • KPI catalogue — the KPI catalogue: every name the simulator emits, with its meaning, grouped by family. A test fails when an emitted name is missing from it.

    • Baselines — the references behind baseline and business_as_usual values.

    • Cost Function — the legacy cost functions of evaluate().

    List the KPIs in your run

    After completing an episode:

    kpis = env.evaluate_v2()
names = kpis[["cost_function", "level", "unit", "description"]].drop_duplicates()
print(names.sort_values("cost_function").to_string(index=False))
kpis.to_csv("kpis.csv", index=False)

    The table starts with cost_function, value, name and level, followed by unit and a short English description from the catalogue. level is building (one row per member, name is the building) or district (one row for the community, name is District). MultiCommunityEnv.evaluate_v2() adds a community_id column and portfolio rows that aggregate the communities’ district rows; the KPIs Reference gives the aggregation rules. Most KPI names end in a unit token such as kwh, kw or ratio. Use the unit column for the display unit. Feature-specific rows follow the scenario configuration, and a KPI that is undefined for the run, such as a service ratio without any event to serve, is missing (NaN) rather than 0 or 1.

    To keep only the default scorecard rows:

    scorecard = env.scorecard(kpis)

    Families

    Every KPI belongs to one of these families. The KPI catalogue lists their names and the KPIs Reference explains each one.

    Family

    Measures

    Cost

    Operating cost, against the conventional baseline and the BAU.

    Grid energy and load shape

    Imports, exports, net exchange, peaks, load factor and ramping.

    Emissions

    Carbon emissions of the grid import.

    Solar self-consumption

    PV generation, export, curtailment and the share used locally.

    Community market

    Local trading, settled and counterfactual costs, savings and local shares.

    Batteries

    Charge, discharge, throughput, equivalent full cycles and capacity fade.

    Electrical service and phases

    Limit violations, requested pressure, phase imbalance and phase peaks.

    Electric vehicles

    Departure service, target accuracy, charging and V2G.

    EV departure audit

    Departure service by feasibility class, with population and coverage counts.

    Flexible appliances

    Completed and missed cycles, start delay and served energy.

    Flexible-appliance audit

    Cycle service from metered energy, with admissibility and coverage counts.

    Escalators

    Passenger service, operating-state changes and electricity use.

    Comfort and resilience

    Thermal discomfort and service during outages, per building.

    Thermal-service audit

    Discomfort and unserved thermal energy pooled over occupied steps.

    Equity

    The distribution of cost benefits between members.

    Equity audit

    The same distribution, with population and coverage counts.

    Demand response

    Requested, delivered and credited response, shortfall and settlement.

    Demand-response audit

    Request service and baseline coverage by request purpose.

    Robustness

    Observation, forecast, action and asset perturbation counters.

    Energy provenance

    Where PV, grid and stored energy went, and the carbon it carried.

    Dynamic topology

    A placeholder row for members that never joined.

    Which KPI to use

    Some quantities have more than one row. Use these for comparisons:

    • Discomfort: district_thermal_service_audit_discomfort_ratio, which pools occupied steps over the members. district_comfort_resilience_* rows are plain means of the building values.

    • Benefit inequality: district_equity_audit_positive_benefit_gini_ratio. It has the same value as district_equity_distribution_gini_benefit_ratio, but its name states that negative benefits are clipped, and its coverage counts are reported with it.

    • Local share of demand: district_solar_self_consumption_community_market_import_share_ratio. district_solar_self_consumption_community_market_local_share_of_demand_ratio has the same formula and is kept as an alias; at building level, local_share_of_demand is the only demand-share row.

    • EV and flexible-appliance service: the audit rows count every departure or cycle and classify its feasibility; the KPIs Reference explains when to read the service rows instead.

    evaluate() retains historical cost-function names. evaluate_v2() is the structured KPI API used by CityLearn v3; its method name is not a software version number.
