    Unreleased

    Added

    • Per-outage-event resilience metrics (opt-in). CityLearnEnv.evaluate_outage_events() returns (events_df, summary_df) and CityLearnEnv.get_outage_resilience_audit() returns the structured audit dictionary (including district outage windows in district_windows) for static-topology episodes (#219). Built directly on citylearn/internal/service_energy.py, each maximal contiguous run of executed steps where simulate_power_outage is enabled and power_outage_signal is finite and nonzero reports start/end steps, duration in steps and hours, expected/served/unserved energy in kWh with per-end-use splits (cooling, heating, dhw, non_shiftable), and peak_unserved_power_kw scaled by seconds_per_time_step. summary_df distinguishes building outage events (building_events_count, building_outage_steps_count, building_outage_hours, worst_building_event_unserved_energy_kwh, fully_served_building_events_ratio) from district outage windows spanning contiguous steps with at least one affected building (district_windows_count, district_window_steps_count, district_window_hours, worst_district_window_unserved_energy_kwh, fully_served_district_windows_ratio). evaluate_v2() and export outputs are unchanged. See the KPIs reference.

    • Recurring evaluation-window KPIs. Opt-in evaluation_window configuration (schema key, CityLearnEnv, MultiCommunityEnv and CLI -ke) adds recurring local-window KPIs without changing simulation physics or existing KPI contracts when disabled: {building,district,portfolio}_energy_grid_window_average_power_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_kw and {building,district,portfolio}_ev_window_net_exchange_control_kwh. Enabling evaluation_window without bounds defaults to 16:00:00 to 19:00:00 in scenario local time, supports sub-hour boundaries, straddling steps and midnight wrap (start > end), records the resolved bounds and time zone in exported_kpis.manifest.json, flags conflicting evaluation windows in compare_manifests(), and keeps shared KPIs paired while noting one-sided windowed rows when comparing an enabled run with a disabled run (#338).

    • Battery health and wear properties and EV battery health KPIs. Battery (and ElectricVehicle.battery) now exposes read-only current_state_of_health, state_of_health, equivalent_full_cycles and capacity_fade_kwh properties, with equivalent_full_cycles computed from accepted gross battery-terminal throughput. CityLearnEnv.evaluate_v2() and MultiCommunityEnv.evaluate_v2() accept include_battery_health_audit: bool = False to append EV battery terminal SOH (*_ev_battery_health_terminal_soh_ratio), capacity fade (*_ev_battery_health_capacity_fade_kwh, *_ev_battery_health_capacity_fade_ratio) and equivalent full cycles (*_ev_battery_health_equivalent_full_cycles_count), attributing building-level EV wear to realized connections and executed transfers and pooling SOH, fade ratios and equivalent full cycles by nominal capacity at district and portfolio levels; see the KPI reference. Disabled by default, so existing KPI tables are unchanged.

    • District feeder limit exceedance monitoring. Opt-in district_electrical_service configuration (schema key and CityLearnEnv argument, with independently optional scalar or dataset-step-0 anchored import_kw and export_kw limits) measures and reports settled district feeder exceedances without enforcing or curtailing device dispatch, so every simulated quantity remains identical to a run without district_electrical_service. Per-step exceedances in kW are exposed on env.feeder_import_exceedance_kw and env.feeder_export_exceedance_kw, evaluate_v2() emits district_energy_grid_feeder_limit_{import,export}_exceedance_energy_total_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_kwh, district_energy_grid_feeder_limit_{import,export}_exceedance_peak_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_kw and district_energy_grid_feeder_limit_{import,export}_exceedance_step_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_count when enabled (undefined for an unconfigured direction), and normalized settings are recorded in effective_schema, physical_configuration_sha256, and compare_manifests() (#334).

    • EV SOC carried across episode splits, opt-in ev_soc_carryover. With an episode split, an EV connected across a boundary started the next episode from the schedule SOC, so the charge of the previous episode was lost. On citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs with the BAU from step 1900, 30 × 24-step episodes met 194 departures under shared and 163 under disjoint, against 213 and 221 in one run over the same rows. With ev_soc_carryover=True (constructor or schema key), an episode that continues the previous one in time starts each EV from the SOC it reached at the end of that episode, and the departure rules count a session that crosses the boundary where it really leaves. The 30 × 24 splits then meet 213 (shared) and 221 (disjoint) departures, and 2 × 100 splits meet 67 under both boundaries, the same as one run. The carried SOC is the session’s start SOC for every EV KPI, including feasibility and the energy accounting. Summed over the episodes, departures, met departures and feasibility classes match one run; the connected SOC gain is each episode’s own gain, so its sum is lower than in one run. Under shared a session reaches the boundary row with the same SOC as in one run, although the controller can act differently at step 0; under disjoint the uncontrolled row between two episodes can leave a session up to one charging step behind. A carried episode starts from the SOC the previous controller left, so the same carried episode run by two controllers is not comparable. Sequential splits carry; random and rolling splits never carry, even when a random pick follows the previous episode, and the env warns once that the setting has no effect there; an explicit list carries only when a window starts exactly where the previous one ends for the split boundary, so under the default shared a list without a gap does not carry (it does under disjoint, or when the next window starts on the previous window’s last row). Only the session connected across the boundary is carried, at the first step; later arrivals take the file SOC, or keep the simulated SOC when the file has none, as in one run. Only the EV SOC is carried; batteries, thermal storage and indoor temperatures still reset. Off, episodes stay independent; on, an episode that continues the previous one depends on the previous controller. The BAU reference starts from the same carried SOC, the setting is recorded in get_metadata() and the export manifest, and compare_exports reports runs with different values as incompatible. The default is off, so results do not change. See the migration guide (#388).

    • HVAC in the BAU. On datasets with HVAC actions (temperature dynamics), the BAU sent zero to heating_device, cooling_device and cooling_or_heating_device, so its buildings were neither heated nor cooled and every *_business_as_usual row compared the controller with unheated or uncooled houses. Under baseline_version=2, the default, the BAU now delivers the dataset’s own heating and cooling demand at each step, clipped to the action bounds and device limits, and on those datasets its definition becomes immediate-full-charge-v4+hvac-dataset-demand-v1. On datasets with HVAC actions the default *_business_as_usual rows and the deltas and ratios to the BAU therefore change; on datasets without HVAC actions nothing changes, bit for bit, including the BAU definition (checked over a full episode of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs and pt_rec_4 with the BAU and the three rule-based baselines). baseline_version=1 keeps the BAU with the HVAC at zero, as in 3.6.0, and BusinessAsUsualAgent(env, hvac=...) overrides the environment for one agent. On Quebec over the first two weeks, the BAU delivers 59,304 kWh of heat instead of 1,725 kWh (the dataset demand is 59,304 kWh), its mean indoor temperature goes from 3.4 °C to 20.6 °C, its district cost from 631.8 to 4,403.8 EUR, and the cost ratio of RBCSmartPolicy to the BAU from 6.970 to 1.000. A controller that does not drive the HVAC, such as ZeroActionAgent, now has a cost ratio of 0.143 there, so read the ratios to the BAU together with the comfort KPIs. See HVAC and the baseline version and the migration guide (#362).

    • evaluate_v2() table from the CLI. citylearn simulate ... evaluate and train --evaluate wrote only the legacy evaluate() KPIs, 21 per entity and mostly ratios to the conventional baseline, so CLI runs had no community market rows, no business-as-usual comparison and none of the KPIs in the KPI reference. They now also write <simulation_id>-evaluation_v2.csv, the evaluate_v2() table with its market and *_business_as_usual_* rows. --no_business_as_usual skips the business-as-usual rollout. <simulation_id>-evaluation.json keeps its format, legacy KPIs included, so existing parsers are unaffected. Simulation results do not change (#367).

    • RLlib wrappers on the current Ray. RLlibSingleAgentWrapper and RLlibMultiAgentEnv are tested with Ray 2.60.0 (new API stack, Python 3.10 to 3.14, gymnasium 1.2.2): PPO and SAC train for a few iterations on one day of citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation, and the trained RLModule produces actions (tests/test_rllib_wrappers.py, skipped without Ray, run in the CI job that installs Ray). RLlibMultiAgentEnv failed on that release at the first reset() because it set neither agents nor possible_agents; it now sets both and the per-agent observation_spaces and action_spaces. RLlibMultiAgentActionWrapper orders the actions by agent ID instead of by the order of the dictionary. A new tutorial, Train a controller with RLlib, trains single-agent and multi-agent PPO controllers and evaluates them with evaluate_v2(); CI executes it. Simulation results are unchanged (#293).

    • KPI units and descriptions. evaluate_v2() and the long multi-community CSV include unit and description from the KPI catalogue. Wide CSV and Parquet exports keep their existing columns and provide the definitions in their manifest (#298).

    • KPI catalogue and complete KPI reference. The KPI catalogue lists every name that evaluate_v2() emits, grouped into 21 families, with its meaning in English and Portuguese. It is generated from citylearn/misc/kpi_catalogue.yml, and a test fails when an emitted name is missing from it. The KPI reference now explains every family, including the audits, energy provenance, the community market, comfort and equity. It gives their equations, district and portfolio aggregation and undefined values, and names the canonical discomfort, Gini and local-share KPIs. Values do not change. (#326)

    • Control values of the grid-shape KPIs. With the BAU comparison, evaluate_v2() now also emits the control value behind each shape delta: district_energy_grid_shape_quality_{peak_daily_average,peak_all_time_average,ramping_average,ramping_total}_control_kw and district_energy_grid_shape_quality_load_factor_penalty_{daily,monthly}_average{,_signed_net}_control_ratio. Existing rows do not change. (#326)

    • Documentation per release. The site home (/, and /stable/) opens the latest final release by default while preserving existing links to shared documentation pages and redirecting development-only root URLs to /dev/<page>; each release tag v* is published under /<version>/, and master is published under /dev/. Every page provides both a top version selector and an Other versions section in the left sidebar driven by /versions.json, keeping the equivalent page open when switching versions or falling back to that version’s home page when missing (docs/publishing.md, #296).

    • Reward versions. Electric_Vehicles_Reward_Function, ComfortReward and SolarPenaltyAndComfortReward accept version, and version 2 follows their documented definitions (#249). In version 1, a building with EV chargers got only its EV terms, a building without chargers got 0, and leaving 0.25 or more below the required departure SOC added weights['soc_under'] ** 2, a bonus of +25 with the default weights; with a required SOC of 0.8, departing at 0.2 scored +15 and departing at 0.65 scored -20. Version 2 adds the MARL base reward to every building and replaces the bonus with min(2 * w, -(w ** 2)) (-25), so the same departures score -35 and -20. In cooling and heating mode, the version 1 comfort reward took heating or cooling from the larger demand at the step, so in heating mode at 21.5 °C with a 20 °C set point it was 0 with the heater off and -1.5 with it on; version 2 takes it from hvac_mode and gives -1.5 in both cases. Version 2 is the default, and every bundled schema that uses these rewards sets "version": 2, so the rewards of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs, the three-phase demos, the 2023 challenge, Quebec and baeda_3dem datasets change; simulations and KPIs do not (#278). Version 1 is deprecated: it exists only to reproduce runs made with CityLearn 3.6.0 or earlier, selecting it raises a DeprecationWarning, and it will be removed in a later release. A single current definition avoids comparing policies trained on different rewards. The reward evidence in the environment metadata and in export manifests records the version. See the migration guide.

    Fixed

    • Dataset cache keyed by content. The dataset cache folder was named only after the release tag, v{__version__}, so every checkout and installation of the same version shared ~/.cache/citylearn/v3.6.0/datasets. A run by dataset name read whichever copy was cached first, even when the local data/datasets had changed: on one development machine, 9 cached schema.json files differed from the checkout, such as the citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs reward without "version": 2. When another process found a copy incomplete, it moved the folder aside and replaced it while environments were still reading it, and their next evaluate_v2() raised Native BAU inputs changed since loading. A complete local copy, such as data/datasets of a checkout or a folder under CITYLEARN_LOCAL_DATASETS_PATH, is now cached in datasets_by_content/<key>/<dataset> of the cache folder, where the key is a digest of the paths and bytes of its files. Different files get a different folder, so a checkout never reads another one’s copy, and a published copy is never replaced while it is in use; only a damaged one, with input files missing, is repaired. The key is computed once per process and again only when a file’s size or modification time changes, so a cache hit costs one stat per file (4 ms on quebec_neighborhood_without_demand_response_set_points). Downloads, used when there is no local copy, stay under datasets/<dataset> of the release tag. Simulation results do not change. See the migration guide.

    • Balance audit tolerance on longer windows. IndependentBalanceAudit compared the district balance residuals, such as grid_balance_residual_kwh, with a fixed 1e-5 kWh. A residual is the window’s source total minus its destination flows, so its rounding grows with those flows: scripts/audit/audit_balances.py on citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo passed over 168 steps and failed over 336, by 1.2e-5 kWh with the BAU and 1.6e-5 kWh with seeded random actions, with the energy balanced. These checks now add flow_relative_tolerance (default 1e-8) times the gross flow of the residual, the sum of the absolute source and destination terms. On that demo the rounding is at most 2e-9 of that flow, so both 336-step runs pass with a margin of at least 5, and a 1e-3 kWh imbalance still fails on any window with less than 99 MWh of gross flow. Other checks and the simulation do not change.

    • Flat wrappers on entity-interface datasets. The pt_rec_* datasets default to interface='entity', and NormalizedObservationWrapper read the entity dictionaries as flat lists: reset() raised TypeError: can't multiply sequence by non-int of type 'float' with central_agent=False and KeyError: 0 with central_agent=True. The other wrappers failed in the same way on every entity environment. The flat-vector wrappers (ClippedObservationWrapper, the normalised, discrete and tabular Q-learning wrappers, the Stable Baselines3 wrappers and the RLlib multi-agent wrappers) now raise a ValueError at construction that names interface='flat'. With interface='flat' the wrappers normalise the pt_rec_* observations as on the other datasets. Observations and simulation results do not change (#351).

    • Occupant set points stay in a plausible range. LogisticRegressionOccupantInteractionBuilding added each occupant change to the current set point with no limit. With set_point_hold_time_steps the set point returns to the schedule after 4 steps without interaction, but under arbitrary HVAC control the occupant keeps interacting, so over the first episode of quebec_neighborhood_with_demand_response_set_points the heating set point random-walked to -2323 °C with random actions and to 3230 °C with BusinessAsUsualAgent, and the observation left its own space (#357). Under physics version 2 an occupant change now stops at set_point_limits, a new building schema key that defaults to [15, 30] °C; a schedule value outside the range is not moved into it. The occupant-interaction delta observation still reports the change the occupant asked for. The set-point observation bounds become the schedule range widened to these limits, [15, 30] °C on both Quebec neighbourhoods, instead of the indoor temperature limits. Physics version 1 is unchanged. With NoControlAgent the set points stay within 16.6 to 26.6 °C and nothing changes. With BusinessAsUsualAgent the simulation and the evaluate() KPIs do not change, but the set-point observations and the rewards that read them do. With random actions the comfort KPIs change and the energy KPIs do not. The LSTM indoor temperature is not limited by this change. See the migration guide (#352).

    • EV arrival SOC of 1 % read as 100 %. Charger files were read value by value: a SOC in [0, 1] was a ratio and one above 1 a percentage, so an arrival SOC of 1 (1 %) in a percent file became 100 % and the EV was not charged. Under physics version 2 each SOC column of a charger file has one unit, decided once from the whole column (percent if any value is above 1), and a charger can declare it with the new soc_unit schema key. The charger files of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs, the three hourly three-phase demos, and the 15-second citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo_15s_parquet and citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_assets_only_demo_15s_parquet contain such rows (8 arrivals in the simulated year of each; citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_asset_changes_demo_15s_parquet has none), so their results change, those of the two 15-second demos too: over the year of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs the BAU charges 534 kWh more (0.58 %), and district import rises by 0.31 % and cost by 0.33 %; the hourly three-phase demos change by 0.31 to 0.38 %. pt_rec_* is unchanged. A warning flags a column read as percent that also holds values between 0 and 1, as one stray value above 1 in a ratio column would cause, and a misspelt charger key such as soc_units is reported. Physics version 1 keeps the old rule. See the migration guide (#368).

    • Charger defaults and misspelt attributes. A charger without max_discharging_power was a 50 kW V2G charger, and one without max_charging_power or efficiency charged at 50 kW with efficiency 1.0; misspelt attribute names were dropped without notice, and the bundled nominal_power and charger_type keys did nothing. Unknown charger attributes now raise a ValueError with a suggestion. Under physics version 2 an omitted max_discharging_power is 0 (no V2G) and an omitted max_charging_power is nominal_power; a warning names every limit that falls back to an assumed value (efficiency only when an efficiency curve does not replace it). charger_type is kept as a label. Every bundled charger sets its limits, so bundled results do not change. Physics version 1 keeps the 50 kW defaults. See the migration guide (#369).

    • EV departures at the end of an episode are counted once. The KPI reference counts a departure at the last step when the EV is still connected and its countdown to departure is in [0, 1], but the code tested the schedule’s terminal row, which the KPI loop never reaches (an episode of N rows has N − 1 control steps). That session was neither counted in ev_events, ev_performance and ev_energy_accounting_connected_soc_gain_kwh nor in the departure audit. The rule now applies at the last step of the member’s KPI window. With split episodes, the next episode used to count such a session at its step 0 with the SOC set at reset or, under episode_split_boundary='disjoint', not at all, and under 'disjoint' it scored a session already connected on the row between the episodes at step 0 with the SOC set at reset. The last step of an episode that another 'disjoint' split follows now also counts a session with countdown 2, which leaves before that split’s first control step, and step 0 skips any session that was connected on the row before it, so each session is counted once, with the SOC the controller reached. Over the 100 steps 1900–1999 of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs with the BAU, Building_1, Building_4 and Building_15 each gain one departure, so the district goes from 32 to 35 departures and its success ratio from 0.844 to 0.857. Over two 100-step episodes from step 1900, one run counts 72 departures, 67 of them met. The two episodes now count 35 + 37 under shared (32 + 40 before), with 64 met (61 before), because the last step scores a session one charging step before it leaves; under disjoint they count 40 + 32 (32 + 37 before), with 67 met (59 before), the same as one run. The default episodes of the bundled EV datasets do not end in such a session, so their KPIs do not change (checked by simulation on citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs, the hourly three-phase demos and pt_rec_4, and on the charger schedules of the other pt_rec_* and the 15-second demos). Simulations do not change. See the migration guide (#370).

    • Multiple storage transfers in one step. Calling Battery.charge() (or StorageDevice.charge()) more than once at the same time_step used to overwrite energy_balance[t] with the last transfer while electricity_consumption[t] summed every transfer, and appended an extra capacity_history entry for each call after the first. StorageDevice._charge() and Battery.charge() now accumulate energy_balance[t] across same-step transfers, add only the incremental transfer to electricity_consumption[t], and update the current step’s capacity_history entry in place with each transfer’s capacity loss. The fix applies to both physics versions 1 and 2; standard simulator dispatch makes at most one transfer per step, so bundled datasets and single-transfer runs are bit-identical under both versions.

    • One power budget per step for repeated storage transfers. Each Battery.charge() call read get_max_input_power() or get_max_output_power() again and got the full nominal power for the step, so several calls in one step could move several times the energy the battery can deliver: three 5 kWh charges put 13.38 kWh through a 5 kW battery in one hour. StorageTank.charge() had the same per-call limit on max_input_power and max_output_power. Both now give one budget per step on terminal energy, the energy at the device’s terminals before conversion losses, and a later call gets only what the earlier calls left. A charge and a discharge in the same step share it: each transfer takes the share of the step its energy is of the power limit in its direction, so |E_in|/P_in + |E_out|/P_out stays within the step duration, and +5 then −5 kWh on a 5 kW battery no longer moves 10 kWh in one hour. For the battery, the budget is read from the power curves at the stored energy before the step’s first transfer, and preview_charge() applies the same remaining budget. Capacity and depth-of-discharge limits still use the current stored energy. The limit is on terminal energy, so a transfer split into several calls is held to the same terminal energy as one call, but with a sloped power_efficiency_curve its stored energy can differ slightly, because each call is converted at its own power. CityLearnEnv.step() makes one transfer per device and step, so simulated results and KPIs do not change; only code that calls charge() more than once in a step sees the limit (#363).

    • District emissions with local trading off. With a community market enabled and local_trading_enabled: false, the settlement, the cost KPIs and the provenance ledger trade no energy locally, but the district emissions still netted one member’s export against another’s import. They now charge every member import at its carbon intensity, the member_sum value, and equal district_energy_provenance_external_purchase_emissions_kgco2 in every market configuration. The local price ratio never affects emissions. Results change only for runs with local trading switched off; no bundled dataset does that. See the migration guide and #327.

    • Invalid community market settings fail at construction. The community_market section was read leniently: an enable typo left the market off, a ratio that was not a number ("0,7") became 0.8, a ratio above 1 was clipped to 1, grid_export_price was ignored unless settlement_version was the number 2 (the string "2" did not count), and weights for unknown buildings or with invalid values were dropped. Each of these now raises a ValueError that names the key, also with enabled: false. The ratio must be a number from 0 to 1 (a value above 1 raises instead of being clipped, because a member cannot pay more for local energy than for grid energy), the two ratio keys must agree when both are set, settlement_version must be the number 1 or 2 (2.0 still selects version 2), a non-zero grid_export_price needs "settlement_version": 2, and member weights and tariff files must name buildings of the schema. evaluate_v2() with the BAU comparison also raises when market attributes were changed on the env after construction, since the native reference reads the schema and would settle a different market. Every bundled schema loads with the same settings, so results do not change. See the migration guide (#366).

    • Portfolio market rows with and without markets. In a MultiCommunityEnv that mixes communities with and without a community market, the portfolio community-market rows summed the zeros that a community without a market reports, so they covered only the market communities while every other portfolio row covered all of them. With two copies of pt_rec_4 over 96 steps, one without a market, the portfolio settled cost was 3.724 EUR against a portfolio cost of 7.449 EUR, and the grid import after local trading was 27.449 kWh against 54.899 kWh imported. A community without a market now enters these portfolio rows with its plain grid settlement: no local trade, its members’ grid import and export, its control cost as settled and counterfactual cost, and zero savings and operator balance. The same portfolio now reports 7.449 EUR settled and 54.903 kWh grid import after local trading (the members’ own imports, before netting), and its shares of local trade are shares of the whole portfolio. KPI names do not change. District rows, simulations and portfolios where every community has a market, or none has, do not change. See the migration guide and #373.

    • Unknown keys in a community entry. MultiCommunityEnv read only the keys it knew in each community entry, so a misspelt key fell back to its default without notice: "wieght": 3.0 left the community at weight 1.0, and "env_kwarg" ran the child with the schema defaults. A boolean weight was also read as 1.0 or 0.0. An entry with a key other than community_id, schema, env_kwargs and weight now raises a ValueError that names the key and lists the accepted ones, and a boolean weight raises a ValueError. Valid configurations and their results do not change (#374).

    • Observation spaces describe the observations. The flat observation space followed active_observations, while the observation vector and observation_names put each building’s EV charger and deferrable appliance features after its other features, so a position could carry the bounds of another feature: on pt_rec_4, Member_001, position 25 is charging_building_headroom_kw (6.8 kW) and had the bounds of an EV state of charge, [-0.1, 1.0]. The space now follows the vector, name by name (Building.flat_observation_order); the observation vector and its names do not change. The entity space now declares meta (an EntityMetaSpace), so observation_space.contains(observations) holds. Three estimated bounds could be exceeded and are widened: per-phase charging headroom, which assumed the phase carried the whole building load; cooling and heating demand of dynamics buildings whose HVAC action sets the demand, which can exceed the dataset demand up to the device output at nominal power; and the set points of occupant-interaction buildings, which occupants move step by step outside the schedule. A test runs an episode on five bundled datasets, flat and entity, central and decentralised, and checks every observation against its space. Simulations and KPIs do not change, except for robustness faults on the flat interface of these datasets, which now reach the feature they name instead of the one at its old position. What bound-based normalisation feeds an agent does change, so agents trained on the affected datasets should be retrained. See the migration guide (#306).

    Physics

    Work package 4 of the 3.6.0 audit (#226), in #279.

    • Versioned physics. physics_version (schema key and CityLearnEnv argument) selects the physics of a run: 1 is the physics of 3.6.0 and earlier, and 2, the default, holds the corrections of the physics work package of the 3.6.0 audit (#226). The BAU reference of evaluate_v2() uses the version of the evaluated environment, and the run metadata of the exports records it. See the migration guide.

    • Standby loss of EVs away from their charger. The away-from-charger SOC drift started from the previous step and discarded that step’s standby loss, so an EV with a non-zero battery loss_coefficient kept its charge while away. Under physics version 2 the drift starts from the SOC after the loss, as it already did for suspended EVs (#236).

    • HVAC demand caps at steps longer than one hour. The cooling and heating demand caps compared an energy per dataset step with the device’s available power, which capped HVAC output at one hour of nominal power per step. Under physics version 2 the heat pump and the electric heater compare energy with energy (#235).

    • Autosizing at sub-hourly resolution. Under physics version 2 storage tanks hold one hour of peak demand power (the duration autosize attribute changes it), so 15-minute data get the same tanks as hourly data instead of a quarter of them, and PV autosizing groups years by the dataset resolution and repeats the hourly PVWatts output at sub-hourly steps, where it used to fail (#233).

    • Battery discharge power. capacity_power_curve describes how charging power tapers near full charge, but it also limited discharge, so a full battery could discharge at only a fraction of its nominal power. Under physics version 2 a new battery attribute, discharge_power_curve, limits discharge; it defaults to a flat curve at nominal_power and can derate at low state of charge, and InventoryBattery keeps its max_discharge_kw (#227).

    • Battery discharge during outages. An islanded battery’s discharge was capped by a pre-step load estimate that ignored the thermal storage discharge and the device limits, so the battery could discharge more than the building consumed and the surplus was reported as PV curtailment, even without PV. Under physics version 2 the battery discharges after the step’s thermal demand and thermal storage discharges and only up to the islanded load that PV leaves, and physics_invariant_checks asserts it (#229).

    • Electrical-service headroom. The import limit of a building with electrical_service was shared among the EV chargers and the battery in proportion to their requested power, including requests the device could not draw (a full battery, a charger without an EV), which curtailed the other devices. Under physics version 2 each request is first limited to the power its device can draw, and the headroom observations and the requested-violation penalty use the same powers (#231).

    • Thermal storage under an electrical-service limit. The headroom was allocated before the step’s thermal storage charging, so the import could exceed the limit while the recorded power, headroom and violation stayed within it. Under physics version 2 the headroom is allocated after the thermal, domestic hot water and non-shiftable updates, and the recorded power, headroom and violation come from the settled import (#230).

    • Deferrable appliance starts. The start check used the realised future outage signal, load, PV and limits, which a controller cannot know, and reported the result through can_start, the action feedback and the refusal of the start. Under physics version 2 a start is decided on the current step and on whether the cycle ends within the episode; a cycle that later meets an outage is served from the islanded supply the other loads leave and, when that supply falls short, stops and is reported as missed (#232).

    • Energy to full of faded batteries. Battery.charge stops at the degraded capacity, which capacity fade lowers below the nominal capacity, but the entity observations computed the energy a battery can still take from the nominal capacity. A battery that fade had made full was therefore observed as able to charge: over the year of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs with the BAU, chargers reported can_charge 1 and a positive available_charge_power_kw at 35,208 charger steps where the EV accepted nothing. The BAU reserved that power, and its home battery discharged for it and exported the energy. Under physics version 2, energy_to_full_kwh, connected_ev_energy_to_full_kwh, flexible_energy_to_full_kwh and the charge features derived from them stop at the lower of the SOC limit and the degraded capacity, for EV and stationary batteries. The simulated physics is unchanged, and batteries without fade, including every pt_rec_* battery, give the same observations. Without the entity_core_electrical bundle no observation shows the fade, so the BAU still estimates the EV headroom from the nominal capacity (see the baselines guide).

    • One-way storage efficiency. StorageDevice.round_trip_efficiency returned the one-way efficiency, the square root of efficiency. one_way_efficiency now holds that value, and round_trip_efficiency keeps it with a DeprecationWarning; the metadata and the entity feature round_trip_efficiency_ratio keep their names and values. Battery.efficiency_history gains one entry per charge or discharge instead of two (#226).

    • Frozen references for both physics versions. The refactor parity audit (scripts/audit/audit_refactor_parity.py) and the NumPy version test (tests/test_numpy_version_independence.py) compared runs only with references recorded under physics version 1, so a change to the default physics passed both checks. Each check now runs under both versions and compares each run with the reference of its version: scripts/audit/snapshots/refactor_parity_physics_v2.json and tests/data/numpy_version_reference_physics_v2.json hold version 2, with the default v2 random streams in the NumPy test, and the version 1 references are unchanged. The NumPy 1.x and 2.x CI jobs must both reproduce each reference. --physics-version selects one version in the audit (#292).

    Physics version 1 reproduces 3.6.0: the BAU and RBCSmartPolicy give bit-identical series and evaluate_v2() tables to 3.6.0 on citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs, citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation, pt_rec_4, pt_rec_30 and the outage week of pt_rec_30/evaluation/rec_30_robustness.json, and the frozen refactor and NumPy references run under it. Under version 2 the discharge curve changes the challenge datasets slightly: over four weeks of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs the BAU imports 0.05% less, and over the 30 days of citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation the BAU costs 0.25% more, because a full battery now discharges at its nominal power. The electrical-service and deferrable corrections leave cost, import and peak of the BAU and RBCSmartPolicy on pt_rec_4 and pt_rec_30 unchanged; they correct the recorded per-phase import peaks of pt_rec_30 (from 19.3 to 17.4 kW on L1 for the BAU, for example), which overstated the settled import, and in the outage week of rec_30_robustness.json the BAU’s unserved deferrable energy falls from 1.47 to 0.28 kWh, because cycles are no longer refused for outages the controller could not know of. The energy-to-full correction changes the BAU on datasets whose EV batteries fade and that publish the entity_core_electrical bundle: over the year of citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs, the BAU’s home batteries export 51 kWh less of what they discharged for full EVs, member import falls by 46 kWh and the cost by 0.03% (32,211.91 to 32,202.41 EUR), and district import rises by 0.03% because that export no longer offsets other members’ import; the EV departure KPIs are unchanged. Over one day of the 15-second three-phase demos, that battery export falls from 3.10 kWh to below 0.01 kWh and the BAU cost rises by 0.08 to 0.09%. pt_rec_* and citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo are unchanged. Rerun results under version 2, or set physics_version=1 to reproduce 3.6.0.

    Random streams and seeds

    Work package 5 of the 3.6.0 audit (#226), in #281. Results change for runs that use random device parameters, electric vehicles away from their charger, occupants or stochastic outages; random_stream_version='v1' restores the 3.6.0 random streams.

    • Random streams are derived per purpose, object and episode. Random device parameters, the state of charge of electric vehicles away from their charger, occupant set-point interactions and stochastic outages reused or shared their random streams (#248). Every unspecified device parameter was the first draw of a new RandomState, so all of them sat at the same quantile; the EV drift seed was random_seed + episode, so seed s in episode 1 repeated seed s + 1 in episode 0, and one stream was shared by every EV; every occupant drew RandomState(max(random_seed, 1) + time_step), the same value in every building and episode; and ReliabilityMetricsPowerOutage skipped day 0 and the partial last day and floored durations. A new random_stream_version setting (schema key and constructor argument) selects the derivation. The default, v2, derives one numpy.random.SeedSequence stream per purpose, object and episode, keeps one generator per stream, and draws outages on every day with durations rounded to the nearest step. v1 reproduces 3.6.0 for the same seed. On citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation the three buildings no longer share identical outages in every episode, and on citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs the 168-hour net consumption changes by −1.6%. Runs without these random features are unchanged. See the migration guide.

    • Agent and neighborhood-generation streams. TabularQLearning drew its exploration decision, and MARLISA with information sharing shuffled its coordination order, with RandomState(random_seed + time_step), and the neighborhood generator seeded partial-load simulation i of building b with b + i, so their streams overlapped across seeds, time steps and buildings (#287). With v2, agents keep one SeedSequence generator per purpose and episode and follow the random_stream_version of their environment, and Neighborhood takes a random_stream_version argument that derives the seed of each partial-load simulation from the neighborhood seed, the building and the simulation index. Agent exploration and newly generated datasets change; simulations and the bundled datasets do not, and v1 restores the 3.6.0 streams.

    • TabularQLearning seeds its action spaces. Random exploration actions, and the fallback action for an observation whose Q-values are all still NaN, were sampled from the gymnasium action spaces of TabularQLearningWrapper, which nothing seeded, so gymnasium seeded them from OS entropy and two runs with the same random_seed did not repeat. The agent now seeds each action space that is not already seeded from random_seed, as SAC does, and a space the caller has seeded keeps its stream. This applies with both random_stream_version values: these actions were never reproducible, so v1 has no stream to preserve.

    • What reset(seed=s) repeats. The migration guide, the CityLearnEnv.reset docstring and EpisodeTracker.random_stream_episode said that reset(seed=s) repeats the first episode of an environment built with random_seed=s. It restarts the per-episode streams (EV drift, occupant interactions, stochastic outages) at the key of episode 0, but the episode window still advances, and device and EV battery parameters and the initial EV state of charge keep the values drawn at construction. The documentation now says so, and recommends building a new environment to repeat a run with another seed and passing a seed to the first reset only, since a seed at every reset repeats the same streams in every episode. The behaviour is unchanged: making a seeded reset rebuild the environment or rewind the window would change every run that seeds its resets (#303).

    • Cloned members draw their own streams. Under a dynamic topology, an add_member event that clones a member from source_member_id renamed the copy but kept the source’s random stream keys, so the clone’s occupant drew exactly the same set-point interactions as its source in every step and episode, and any later draw from its devices repeated the source’s. The clone and everything under it (occupant, devices, chargers, appliances) are now keyed by the new member’s name. Results change only for schemas that clone members with occupants or other random draws, under random_stream_version='v2'; v2 is not yet released, v1 is unchanged, and no bundled dataset clones a member. Device parameters drawn at construction are still copied from the source (#304).

    Physics and KPIs:

    • Power and energy at sub-hourly steps. Several limits compared a power in kW with an energy per step in kWh, which only coincide at 3600 s. available_nominal_power is now a power at every step length; heat pumps and electric heaters no longer exceed nominal_power when they charge thermal storage; on native sub-hourly data a device delivers at most nominal_power x step duration per step; during an outage, the local supply passed to devices and to the non-shiftable load is converted to power and to dataset resolution; and the cooling_demand, heating_demand and dhw_demand observations convert the storage discharge to dataset resolution when time_step_ratio is not 1. (#185)

    • Undersized devices. A device that cannot meet its demand no longer raises AssertionError: demand is greater than ..._device max output. Its output is clipped, a RuntimeWarning is issued once per device and episode, and the shortfall is reported as unserved thermal service energy. (#185)

    • Base-power estimate. The estimate behind electrical-service headroom and the feasibility KPIs no longer subtracts thermal storage discharge twice, and it counts escalator power, including a requested escalator state in the step it is requested. (#185)

    • Clip reasons. clip_reason_phase_headroom and clip_reason_export_headroom name only the asset’s own phases and the clipped direction. (#185)

    • Normalized observations. observations(normalize=True, periodic_normalization=False) no longer raises KeyError: 'month'. (#185)

    • Occupant set-point hold. set_point_hold_time_steps in a building schema now reaches LogisticRegressionOccupantInteractionBuilding. On the Quebec neighbourhood datasets (4 steps), occupant set-point changes revert to the schedule after 4 steps without interaction instead of holding for the rest of the episode. (#185)

The district reference series summed only the members active at evaluation time, while the control series keep every member’s past consumption, so a departed member counted on the control side only. With all-zero actions on citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_topology_demo (24 steps, Building_6 leaving at step 12), the district import delta to baseline was 12.76 kWh, the export delta -11.00 kWh and the emissions delta 4.44 kg, although control and baseline coincide; they are now below 4e-6, the float32 rounding of the reference series. With noise_std > 0, Gaussian noise could make solar generation, solar irradiance and carbon intensity negative, add generation at night and push outdoor relative humidity outside [0, 100] %. One rule now bounds the noisy values: solar quantities stay >= 0 and get no noise at night, humidity stays within [0, 100] % and carbon intensity stays >= 0. The noise draws are unchanged, so values within range are bit-identical.     • Battery discharge features during outages. These features now report the battery’s discharge capacity without the grid export headroom, as #205 does for vehicle-to-building discharge; the simulator still limits the discharge to the local load. The rule-based baselines read can_discharge, so they now use the battery during outages. In the week with the most outages of pt_rec_30/evaluation/rec_30_robustness.json and rec_30_combined.json, the BAU discharges 1.2 kWh more and the normalized unserved energy during outages falls from 0.0015 to 0.0007 and from 0.0017 to 0.0008; cost and emissions are unchanged. This closes #220. (#221)

    • Baselines without the entity_core_electrical bundle. Two changes apply only to datasets whose schema does not enable that bundle; pt_rec_* and citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs enable it and are unchanged. First, the BAU battery reserved the charger’s nominal power for a connected EV even when the EV accepted much less, for example in the last step before 100% SOC, and discharged for that demand; it now reserves the power the EV can accept, estimated from the observed SOC, battery capacity, charger power and efficiency as the simulator computes available_charge_power_kw (#190). Second, every baseline took the last interval’s export as PV surplus, so when a battery discharge overshot the load and was exported, the battery recharged from the grid in the next step (22.8 kWh in the first 48 hours of citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo with the BAU); only PV above the observed load now counts as surplus. The definition versions become immediate-full-charge-v3, immediate-user-target-v2, basic-v3, smart-local-v3 and community-v3. Over 30 days, the control cost changes by −0.2% to −2.4% and the emissions by −0.8% to −2.5% for every baseline on citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all, citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation and citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo, except the default BAU on the last one, which costs 0.4% more because its battery now stores PV that previously went to the community market. See the migration guide. The first change addresses consequence 2 of #190; consequence 1, the one-step battery lag, is unchanged, and #190 stays open. (#213)

    • Baselines read corrupted observations under the flat interface. Under the flat interface the env corrupts only the flat vectors, and the baselines, which read entity observations, decided from uncorrupted values; under the entity interface they read the corrupted ones (#192). Run as controllers (predict(observations)), they now get the step’s robustness observation events applied to their entity observations as the entity interface applies them, and take the same actions under both interfaces. No random stream is consumed, and the env’s flat observations and robustness history are unchanged; the controller KPIs of flat runs with observation events can change. The native BAU reference of evaluate_v2() (predict([])) stays uncorrupted. See the migration guide. (#212)

    Agents and wrappers (#208):

    • TabularQLearning no longer bootstraps from the next state on terminal transitions.

    • MARLISARBC explores with its RBC; it used to explore with random actions.

    • MARLISA with pca_compression < 1 sizes its networks to the PCA output instead of failing at the first update.

    • Discrete wrappers. TabularQLearningObservationWrapper declares exactly the prod(bins) states it encodes (it declared prod(bins + 1) - 1 and could encode a state outside the space), and DiscreteObservationWrapper keeps values above the observation space in the top bin. DiscreteObservationWrapper and DiscreteActionWrapper bin the spaces of the env they wrap, so they work over the normalized wrappers. ClippedObservationWrapper no longer modifies the environment’s cached observations.

    • OnehotEncoding raises ValueError for a value outside its classes.

    Data loading (#209):

    • Charger files with blank or float states. Charger states are parsed as numbers. A blank cell used to make every state of the charger unreadable, so no EV connected.

    • InventoryBattery EVs load without nominal_power, which the battery derives from max_charge_kw and max_discharge_kw.

    • Random initial EV SOC is drawn within the battery’s declared min_soc and max_soc; an InventoryBattery used to fail when the draw from [0, 1] fell outside them.

    • Entity observations with max_soc < 1 report soc_max_ratio, the energy to full and the available charge power up to max_soc instead of a full battery.

    Datasets and dataset cache:

    • Incomplete dataset caches. A cached dataset counted as complete as soon as its schema.json existed, so a cache left by an interrupted download raised FileNotFoundError for its first missing file. A cache is now complete only when every input file that CityLearnEnv reads from the schema exists; an incomplete cache is replaced in one step, and offline mode names the missing files (#176, #181).

    • Dataset environment variables. CITYLEARN_OFFLINE, CITYLEARN_DATASET_GITHUB_ACCOUNT, CITYLEARN_DATASET_REPOSITORY and CITYLEARN_DATASET_TAG are read when CityLearnEnv or DataSet is created instead of once at import, and CityLearnEnv(offline=None) honours CITYLEARN_OFFLINE (#198).

    • Dataset names. DataSet.get_dataset_names() keeps a dataset whose name occurs in its parent path and accepts a datasets_path with a trailing slash; concurrent first calls list GitHub once and write the cached list atomically. The misc files share the dataset cache code (#199).

    • 15 s Parquet datasets load from the dataset cache. The schemas of citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo_15s_parquet, citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_asset_changes_demo_15s_parquet and citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_assets_only_demo_15s_parquet set a repository-relative root_directory. Passing the schema path returned by DataSet().get_dataset(name) to CityLearnEnv resolved it against the cache folder and raised FileNotFoundError for <cache>/<dataset>/data/datasets/<dataset>/Building_1.parquet (#194). The three schemas now set "root_directory": null, like the other datasets. CityLearnEnv(name) and schema paths inside a repository checkout already found the files and still read the same folder. (#197)

    Exports, CLI and files (#207, #202, #203):

    • BAU time-series export. The BAU export writes the executed time steps only, like the control exports. It used to add the terminal step, a copy of the last executed step, which inflated totals summed from the file.

    • Charger exports. Without an EV, the charger row writes -1.00 under EV SOC-% instead of adding an EV SOC column.

    • CLI evaluation. citylearn simulate ... evaluate and train --evaluate no longer fail (All arrays must be of the same length, or not enough values to unpack with -te), and train --evaluate evaluates the trained agent instead of a new one.

    • Schema agent attributes. load_agent() without agent keeps schema['agent']['attributes'] when keyword arguments such as env= are passed; keyword arguments override them. Simulator(..., agent_name=None) therefore builds the schema’s agent with the schema’s parameters.

    • Windows. run_work_order() activates virtual environments with activate.bat, which cmd.exe can run. JSON and YAML files are read and written as UTF-8, so non-ASCII text in schemas is no longer decoded as cp1252.

    • Multi-community KPI comparison. load_kpis() and compare_exports() read multi-community KPI exports, which repeat each name once per community, instead of raising Duplicate KPI/name rows.

    • JSON files with NumPy values. FileHandler.write_json writes NumPy booleans, integers and floats as JSON booleans and numbers, and NumPy arrays of booleans and numbers as lists, instead of their text (2.5 instead of "2.5", [1.0, null] instead of "[ 1. nan]"). With ignore_nan=False, a NaN NumPy float raises ValueError like a Python float. namedtuples and Decimal values are written as before, and the export manifests are unchanged. See the migration guide. (#203)

    • pandas 3. Exports no longer emit the two pandas 3 deprecation warnings, and a Parquet render column that mixes text and numbers is written instead of raising ArrowTypeError. Export files are unchanged (#202).

    Performance

    None of these changes a result; each PR compared trajectories and KPIs bit for bit with master.

    • Single time steps. Per-step code reads one time step of device and storage series instead of rebuilding the whole series, and evaluate_v2() builds its records without DataFrame scans (#183).

    • Time-series windows. TimeSeriesData keeps each variable’s episode window as a plain attribute, so reading weather, pricing, carbon, charger and energy-simulation values no longer goes through a Python __getattribute__ and a new slice per read (#210).

    New opt-in features

    These are off by default; existing schemas and results are unchanged.

    • Reward for load left unserved during power outages. During an outage a building cannot import, so its net consumption is zero and rewards built on grid import give no signal about the load that PV and storage leave unserved. OutageUnservedEnergyReward(coefficient, base_reward) subtracts coefficient times that unserved energy from a base reward, and the new flat observation power_outage_unserved_energy (kWh per step, last settled step) gives it per building with the definition of the power_outage_normalized_unserved_energy_total KPI (#188). The reward works under both interfaces; the observation is flat only. Both are opt-in, so existing schemas and results are unchanged. (#195)

    • Time-varying available grid power. buildings.<name>.electrical_service.available_grid_power references a per-step series of the power a building may import, in kW, from a file with one column per building or a named column (#119). The import limit in force at a step is the smaller of the static limits.total.import_kw and the series value, and every reader of the total import limit uses it: the electrical-service allocation (EV chargers and the battery are curtailed first; fixed load stays served and counts as a violation), deferrable start feasibility, the headroom observations, the EV feasibility KPI and the electrical-service violation KPI. A limit of 0 kW does not island the building; outages stay in power_outage. The new flat observation available_grid_power reports the limit for the coming interval when a series is set, and the rule-based baselines cap their planned import headroom with it. Buildings without a series are unchanged. See the schema reference. (#216)

    • Vehicle-to-building backup during outages (opt-in). With power_outage.vehicle_to_building: true, a connected EV may discharge to its building while the grid is down, as a vehicle-to-home backup. The discharge is limited to the non-shiftable load and the thermal device load that PV and the battery leave uncovered, so the building exports nothing to the failed grid. Under the entity interface, the charger’s can_discharge and available_discharge_power_kw report the vehicle’s discharge capacity during the outage. The discharge can take the vehicle below its required departure SOC; that is left to the controller and shows in the EV departure KPIs. The option is off by default, and results without it are unchanged. See the migration guide. (#205)
