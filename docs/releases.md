# Releases

Changes, compatibility, validation and measured performance for the current
CityLearn release. Older notes are retained in the [release archive](releases_archive.md).

Portuguese version: [pt/releases.md](pt/releases.md).

For package publication, version policy and the release checklist, see the
[publishing guide](publishing.md).

## Unreleased

### Changed

- **CityLearn 3.6.0 compatibility versions.** `physics_version` 1, `baseline_version` 1, `random_stream_version` `v1` and version 1 of `Electric_Vehicles_Reward_Function`, `ComfortReward` and `SolarPenaltyAndComfortReward` existed only to reproduce CityLearn 3.6.0, and every later correction had to keep a version 1 branch and a version 1 frozen reference. They are removed. Each of these settings accepts only version 2 (`v2`), its default, and any other value raises a `ValueError` that says version 1 was removed in 3.8.1 and that CityLearn 3.7 reproduces 3.6.0. Runs still record `physics_version`, `baseline_version` and `random_stream_version` in their metadata and export manifests, so their exports stay comparable with those of earlier releases. Reward objects pickled by 3.6.0, which have no version attribute, now compute version 2. `BusinessAsUsualAgent(env, hvac='off')` still runs the BAU with the HVAC at zero. The version 1 references of the refactor parity audit and of the NumPy version test are removed ([#466](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/466)).
- **Deprecated names.** The agent aliases `BaselineAgent` and `ZeroActionBaselineAgent` (use `NoControlAgent` and `ZeroActionAgent`), `StorageDevice.round_trip_efficiency` and the `round_trip_efficiency` key of device metadata (use `one_way_efficiency`), the misspelt `action_scaling_coefficienct` keyword of the `RLC` agents (use `action_scaling_coefficient`; the misspelling now raises `TypeError` instead of being ignored), and the unused `DataSet.GITHUB_API_CONTENT_URL` and `DataSet.DEFAULT_CACHE_DIRECTORY` are removed. The entity `storage` feature `round_trip_efficiency_ratio` is renamed `one_way_efficiency_ratio`, with the same value; controllers and saved normalisers that read it by name need the new name. The legacy `evaluate()` and `citylearn/cost_function.py` remain supported ([#466](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/466)).
- **One rule for the versioned settings.** `physics_version`, `baseline_version`, `random_stream_version`, the reward `version` and `community_market.settlement_version` were parsed differently: `2.0` was accepted by three of them and rejected by two, and `"2"` the other way round, so a schema that wrote every version the same way could fail on one key only. One parser now reads all five: a version is an integer or a string of digits (`2` or `"2"`), and `random_stream_version` also takes its name (`"v2"`). Floats, `2.0` included, and booleans raise a `ValueError` that names the setting and the accepted values, so a schema that wrote `2.0` must write `2`. The [schema reference](schema_reference.md#versions) lists the five settings in one table ([#469](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/469)).
- **Portfolio audit rates are pooled.** `MultiCommunityEnv.evaluate_v2()` gave the EV departure, thermal-service and flexible-appliance audit ratios, the equity audit coverage and losers percentage, the useful-PV ratios and the stationary-battery capacity fade as means weighted by community `weight`, although the table holds their counts, energies and capacities: two communities with 2 departures (none met) and 200 departures (all met) gave 0.5 instead of 200/202, and a community with weight 0 counted in the counts but not in the ratio. These rows are now pooled from their denominators, like the other portfolio rates, and their BAU deltas and ratios compare the pooled values. The `storage` and `bus` max-residual diagnostics of the provenance ledger take the largest community value instead of the sum. Grid-shape ratios, the equity Gini and top-20 % share, the legacy comfort rows and the `building_mean` ratios stay weighted means. Each portfolio row now has its own `description`, which states its rule before the district definition ([#428](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/428)).
- **Portfolio KPI exports have a manifest, and KPI exports can include the battery-health audit.** `MultiCommunityEnv.export_final_kpis()` now writes `exported_kpis_multi_community.manifest.json` with the community ids and weights, the settings the communities share, the export settings, the rule of each portfolio row, the KPI definitions and each community's manifest path and comparison evidence, and writes the portfolio table as Parquet when every community uses `render_file_format='parquet'`. `compare_exports()` pairs two portfolio exports community by community instead of returning `status='unknown'`. `MultiCommunityEnv` gains `set_experiment_identity()` and `scorecard()` (with `level='portfolio'`). `export_final_kpis()` of both environments takes `include_battery_health_audit`, and the manifests record it with `include_business_as_usual` and `kpi_round_decimals` under `kpi_export`; two exports rounded differently compare as incompatible ([#429](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/429)).
- **KPI rows have a status.** The KPI catalogue marks legacy rows (older definitions that an audited row now answers: the `ev_performance` departure rates, the legacy EV departure counts, the overall and outage comfort rows, the flexible-appliance service counts, level and energies) and deprecated aliases (the equity distribution Gini, top-20 % share and losers percentage, and the district `local_share_of_demand` market share), each with its canonical row. `evaluate_v2()` has two new columns, `status` (`current`, `canonical`, `legacy` or `deprecated`) and `replacement`, which the export manifests also record in `kpi_definitions`, and the KPI catalogue page marks the same rows. No row is removed ([#433](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/433)).
- **Exported timestamps follow the calendar columns.** Without a calendar file, exports stamped row 0 at midnight of `start_date` (2024-01-01 by default), while the evaluation window and the calendar observations read the `hour` column: the 2022-based datasets start at 23:00 on a Sunday in July and exported 2024-01-01T00:00, one hour ahead of their `hour` at every row, and the Portugal datasets exported UTC times against Lisbon civil hours. The time of day now comes from the `hour` (hour 1 is 00:00), `minutes` and `seconds` columns; the date is `start_date` or, without one, the date that the `month` and `day_type` columns give; with a `timezone`, timestamps are in that zone with their UTC offset. The 2022-based and three-phase datasets now start at 2022-07-31T23:00, the 2020 climate zones and the Alameda, Travis and Chittenden neighbourhoods at 2018-01-01, the 2021 dataset at 2015-01-01, the 2023 datasets at 2018-06-01 or 2021-06-01, `baeda_3dem` at 2020-06-01, the Quebec datasets at 2018-01-01, and the Portugal datasets at 2023-01-01T00:00+00:00 in Lisbon time. In `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, row 0 is restored to `month=7`, `hour=24`, `day_type=7`, as in the other 2022 datasets, instead of the `hour=0` outside the 1-24 range that made a 25-row first day, and its `start_date` of 2024-08-01, a Thursday against `day_type=1`, is removed. The same actions give the same simulated energy; the calendar observations of row 0, and the actions of rule-based agents that read them at that step, change ([#460](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/460)).
- **Independent random streams for each community.** Random streams were keyed by seed, purpose, entity and episode, so communities of a `MultiCommunityEnv` with the same seed and the same member or EV names drew identical EV drift, outages, occupant interactions, drawn device parameters, EV initial SOC and noise. Each community now passes its `community_id` as the new constructor option `random_stream_namespace`, which is added to every stream key; the shared seed still selects the same episode windows. A standalone `CityLearnEnv` has no namespace, and its streams do not change. The namespace is recorded in the metadata and the comparison context, and runs with different namespaces compare as incompatible ([#462](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/462)).
- **Calendar files are checked at load.** A `time_axis.calendar_file` was checked for regularity only. It must now have a row for every global row up to `simulation_end_time_step` (the last one may be missing with `terminal_observation_padding`), and, converted to `timezone`, give the `month`, `hour`, `minutes` and `seconds` of the data, or construction raises a `ValueError` that names the first row that differs; a `day_type` that is not the weekday gives a warning. Calendars ending in `.parq`, or in any case of `.parquet` and `.pq`, are read as Parquet, and a `timestamp_local` without a UTC offset is read as wall-clock time in `timezone` instead of being rejected ([#464](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/464)).

### Fixed

- **Thermal storage charging during an outage.** In an islanded step a charging cooling, heating or DHW storage ran right after its own device, before the other devices and the non-shiftable load, and could take the supply they needed: a battery discharging exactly the islanded load could end up in a tank while the non-shiftable load was reported as unserved. A charging thermal storage now runs after the non-shiftable load, so, like the charging battery, it takes only the supply that the cooling, heating and DHW devices and the non-shiftable load leave. Runs without outages, and outage steps without a thermal storage charge, do not change ([#420](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/420)).
- **Entity BESS and EV feasibility features.** The battery uses the square root of its round-trip efficiency for each conversion, but the entity `storage` features divided or multiplied by the whole round-trip efficiency, so with 0.81 and 1 kWh above the floor `available_discharge_power_kw` read 0.81 kW for one hour while the battery can deliver 0.90 kW. They now use the one-way efficiency read from `power_efficiency_curve` at the power the battery applies. The EV features compared the stored energy with the AC charger power without any efficiency. `required_average_power_kw`, `avg_power_to_departure_kw`, `charging_slack_kw`, `charging_priority_ratio` and `min_required_action_normalized` are now AC-side, at the charger: the stored energy divided by the charger efficiency times the EV battery's one-way efficiency, both at full charger power, and `max_deliverable_energy_until_departure_kwh`, `departure_energy_margin_kwh` and `departure_feasibility_ratio` use the same conversion. The [data unit contract](data_unit_contract.md#ev-flexibility-features) and the [observations reference](observations_reference.md) describe the AC side, and that `max_*_energy_kwh_step` uses the SOC-dependent power limit. Controllers that read these features see larger required powers. The rule-based baselines add their service margin only to their own battery-side estimate of the required rate, since the AC-side feature already includes the losses; on the first week of `pt_rec_4` and `pt_rec_15` and 720 hours of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` their costs change by less than 0.01% and they meet the same EV departures. `EV_LAST_INTERVAL_LOSS_ALLOWANCE` stays 1.25: it is still needed without the `entity_core_electrical` observations and for the lower part-load efficiency of the last interval ([#421](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/421)).
- **Battery and charger curves.** `power_efficiency_curve`, `capacity_power_curve` and `discharge_power_curve` of a battery and `charge_efficiency_curve` and `discharge_efficiency_curve` of a charger were stored without checks, and the battery curves were extrapolated linearly outside their points: `[[0, 0.85], [0.5, 0.95]]` as an efficiency curve stopped a run with an `AssertionError` at the first full-power step, and a charger curve reaching 1.05 created energy. Each curve now needs at least two points with `x` strictly increasing within [0, 1], efficiencies in (0, 1] and power fractions >= 0; otherwise construction raises a `ValueError` that names the attribute path, such as `buildings.Building_1.electrical_storage.attributes.power_efficiency_curve`. Outside their first and last points the battery curves keep their end values, as the charger curves (`np.interp`) already did. Curves that cover [0, 1], as in every bundled dataset, give the same results ([#423](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/423)).
- **Charger efficiency at the applied power.** With a `charge_efficiency_curve` or `discharge_efficiency_curve`, the charger read the efficiency at the commanded power and applied it to the energy the EV battery accepted, so when the battery limited the transfer (near full, near its floor or at its power curve) the AC energy and the charger losses were computed at the wrong point of the curve. The efficiency is now read at the AC power actually applied, by fixed-point iteration, in `update_connected_electric_vehicle_soc` and in `preview_power_kw`. Chargers with a constant efficiency, and transfers the battery does not limit, do not change ([#424](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/424)).
- **Topology override of battery efficiency.** An `add_asset` event for `electrical_storage` with `"overrides": {"efficiency": ...}` was accepted but had no effect: the value was appended to the efficiency history, which `reset()` cut back to the source battery's value, and the simulation read the source's `power_efficiency_curve`. The override now sets the rated efficiency, as autosizing does, and rescales a generated `power_efficiency_curve`, so it takes effect and survives a reset. When the source battery has a `power_efficiency_curve` from its schema, an `efficiency` override without a `power_efficiency_curve` override raises a `ValueError` at construction that says to override the curve. The bundled `pt_rec_30` and `pt_rec_100` topology events override both, so their batteries now report the overridden 0.94 as their rated efficiency (`efficiency_ratio`) after a reset ([#425](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/425)).
- **`loss_coefficient` units.** The storage `loss_coefficient` was set as an hourly fraction, as the schema documents, but read back as the compounded fraction per control step, and `get_metadata()` exported that per-step value under the schema key, so the metadata could not be fed back into a schema, and re-assigning the value read back changed the physics (at 15-minute steps 0.009 per hour became 0.00226 per hour). `loss_coefficient` now reads back the configured hourly value, the new `step_loss_fraction` gives the loss per control step, and device metadata exports both. `env.get_metadata()['temporal_reproducibility']` declares `storage_loss_coefficient: fraction_per_hour` and `storage_step_loss_fraction: fraction_per_control_step`. Simulation results do not change ([#426](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/426)).
- **Topology events are checked at construction.** Events naming members that `buildings=[...]` did not load were accepted and then raised a `ValueError` when they fired (`rec_30_topology.json` with `buildings=['Member_002', 'Member_003']` stopped at step 4080), override keys of a charger or deferrable appliance added without `source_asset_id` were not checked, and `remove_asset` with a misspelt charger or appliance id or `add_asset` with an id that already existed did nothing without a warning. Every event that can fire in the simulation window is now replayed at construction: member ids must name a loaded member or one cloned by an earlier `add_member`, asset events must target an active member, a removed charger or appliance must exist, an added one must be new, and the source asset (`source_asset_id`, else `target_asset_id`, as the event resolves it) must exist and takes the override check. Each failure raises a `ValueError` that names the event. Events after `simulation_end_time_step` are not checked ([#461](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/461)).
- **`day_type` of the Portugal REC datasets.** The member files of `pt_rec_4`, `pt_rec_15`, `pt_rec_30` and `pt_rec_100` coded each member's rest days (weekends, holidays and, for some members, school breaks or shift rest days) as 1 and the other days as the weekday plus 2, so Monday to Friday read 2 to 6 instead of CityLearn's 1 Monday to 7 Sunday. The entity feature `is_weekend` (`day_type` 6 or 7) therefore marked Fridays as weekend days and rest days, Saturdays and Sundays included, as working days, and `day_type`, `day_type_sin` and `day_type_cos` meant something different for each member. The 388 member files now hold the Lisbon weekday, the same for every member, with 8 on the 21 public holidays of 2023 and 2024 that fall from Monday to Friday (the national holidays and 13 June, the Lisbon municipal holiday); a holiday on a Saturday or Sunday keeps 6 or 7. Nothing else in the files changes. Controllers see the corrected `day_type` and `is_weekend`; the physics and the KPIs do not change (the BAU and `RBCSmartPolicy` give the same `evaluate_v2()` tables before and after on the first week of `pt_rec_4` and on the week of 25 April 2023). `scripts/manual/rewrite_pt_rec_day_type.py` records the rewrite, and `scripts/generate_annual_rec_suite.py` now also writes 8 on its holidays from Monday to Friday.

## v3.8.0 - 2026-11-27

### What's new

3.8.0 makes CityLearn follow the Gymnasium API at the end of an episode and fixes the reinforcement-learning, KPI and documentation issues that the audit of 3.7.0 left open and that are small enough to change now. Every CityLearn episode ends because its window or split ends, a time limit after which the buildings, batteries and EVs would carry on, so the last step now returns `terminated=False, truncated=True`; learners that follow the Gymnasium API, including the bundled SAC, MARLISA and Q-learning agents, Stable Baselines3 and RLlib, keep bootstrapping from the last observation instead of treating the energy left in storage as worthless. In addition, seeded random actions repeat, the CLI records the actions the environment applied, the rewards reject misspelt arguments, discrete agents can leave storage idle, peak ratios of an exporting district read correctly, and `MultiCommunityEnv` checks more of the settings its portfolio adds up. The same actions give the same simulation results as in 3.7.0, apart from the peak ratio rows and the padded terminal row described below.

### Changed

- **The end of an episode is reported as truncation.** Every CityLearn episode ends because its window or episode split ends, which is a time limit, but `step()` returned `terminated=True, truncated=False` on the last step. Learners that follow the Gymnasium API, including the bundled SAC, MARLISA and Q-learning agents, Stable Baselines3 and RLlib, then treated the last observation of every episode as an absorbing state with no future value, so the energy left in batteries and EVs at the end of an episode was learned as worthless. The last step now returns `terminated=False, truncated=True`, and these learners keep bootstrapping from it. Simulation results and KPIs do not change; trained policies can. The new `env.done` property says whether the episode is over, and the `env.terminated` property keeps that meaning, so loops written as `while not env.terminated:` keep working; a loop on the `terminated` flag returned by `step()` alone must also check `truncated` ([#435](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/435)).
- **Seeded random actions repeat.** The central flat action space, `NormalizedActionWrapper`, `DiscreteActionWrapper` and the SB3 wrapper returned a new space on every access of `action_space`, so `env.action_space[0].seed(s)` had no lasting effect, and the base `Agent` sampled from unseeded spaces. The same space object is now returned while its bounds do not change, and every agent seeds its action spaces from `random_seed` (a space the caller has already seeded keeps its stream), so random-controller runs and seeded `action_space.sample()` calls repeat ([#441](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/441)).
- **CLI evaluation records the applied actions, and SB3 training runs whole episodes.** `simulate ... evaluate` recorded the agent's raw output, so with `-w` action wrappers it stored bin indices or normalised values, and it failed with `TabularQLearningWrapper` when a building had more than one action. It now records the actions the environment applied after the wrappers. SB3 training counted data rows instead of control steps (`episodes * time_steps`), so every run ended with a partial extra episode; it now trains `episodes * (time_steps - 1)` steps ([#439](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/439)).
- **Reward arguments are checked.** `RewardFunction` and `OutageUnservedEnergyReward` ignored keyword arguments they did not use, so a misspelt schema attribute had no effect. The rewards of `citylearn.reward_function` now raise a `TypeError` that names the closest valid argument; reward classes defined elsewhere still receive extra keys. `Electric_Vehicles_Reward_Function`, the only reward that uses `charging_constraint_penalty_coefficient`, now accepts it instead of raising, `OutageUnservedEnergyReward` passes its other keys to the base reward as documented, and the reward evidence of the export records only the parameters the reward uses ([#438](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/438)).
- **Discrete actions include idle.** `DiscreteActionWrapper` mapped its default 10 bins to `np.linspace(low, high, 10)`, which has no 0 on [-1, 1], so a discrete or tabular agent could never leave a battery, thermal storage or V2G charger idle. The default is now 11 bins, and when `low < 0 < high` the grid point closest to 0 is exactly 0 for any bin count ([#440](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/440)).
- **Documentation of the RL components.** The `action_scaling_coefficient` of SAC and MARLISA (0.5 by default) caps every action, including HVAC and EV charging, at half its range, which the docstring now says ([#442](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/442)). The `SolarPenaltyReward` docstring says that a building without storage always gets 0 and that a building with several storage types counts its import once per storage type, and the `higher_exponent` default of `SolarPenaltyAndComfortReward` reads 2.0, as in the code ([#443](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/443)). The observation limits come from the simulation window, as the code comment now says, and the Stable Baselines3 tutorial builds its training and evaluation environments on the same two weeks, each running its own week, so both normalise the observations alike ([#437](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/437)).

### Fixed

- **Peak ratios of an exporting district.** Peaks use signed net power, so a district that exports at every step of a window has a negative peak, and the plain ratio read a higher control peak as an improvement: a BAU daily-peak average of −4 kW and a control of −2 kW gave 0.5. The `evaluate_v2()` peak ratios to the baseline and to the BAU now use the signed ratio, `1 + (control − reference) / |reference|` for a negative reference, as net exchange and cost already do; the legacy `evaluate()` rows keep the plain ratio ([#430](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/430)).
- **`MultiCommunityEnv` compares more settings.** The portfolio adds up the EV departure counts and the provenance totals of every community, so communities must now also share `ev_departure_service_tolerance`, `ev_departure_within_tolerance` and `energy_provenance`; with different values the constructor raises a `ValueError` ([#431](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/431)).
- **Calendar of the padded terminal row.** With `terminal_observation_padding`, the extra terminal row advanced the hour as 0-23 even in files that use 1-24, changed the month only from December and mapped the holiday `day_type` 8 to 2. It now copies the time of day from the row one day earlier, so each file keeps its own hour convention, takes the weekday from the row one week earlier when that is not a holiday, and advances the month only when the data show that the month has ended ([#463](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/463)).
- **KPI units.** The thermal-service degree-hours carry the unit `degC_h` instead of `hours`; the equivalent full cycles keep the unit `count` that their names end with ([#434](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/434)).
- **pandas 2.2 or newer.** Loading dynamic-topology member files uses a pandas option that exists only from pandas 2.2, so an older pandas failed with `OptionError`; `requirements.txt` now requires `pandas>=2.2` ([#465](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/465)).
- **Release checks.** The PyPI workflow fails when the release tag differs from `v` + `__version__`, and a manual run must start from a tag ([#470](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/470)). The unused `tests/unit/.coveragerc`, with one machine's absolute paths, and `citylearn.utils.paths.PROJECT_ROOT` are removed ([#472](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/472)).
- **Documentation.** The physics-version table and the HVAC section say that the one-hour device cap of version 1 applies to LSTM dynamics buildings only, and list the clipping of out-of-range flat actions and the cycle-based bounds of the flat deferrable `*_kwh` observations; the action reference describes the version 2 step order; the `ElectricHeater` docstring gives the real defaults ([#427](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/427)). The scorecard guide selects the audited EV departure rows and links the shared rules for undefined ratios ([#432](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/432)). The `per_kw` PV formula of the data unit contract includes the step duration ([#422](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/422)), and the RLlib tutorial installs CityLearn from PyPI ([#444](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/444)).

### Validation

On the release pull request, the full test suite passed on every CI job (Python 3.10 to 3.14 with NumPy 2, and Python 3.10 with NumPy 1), together with the runtime audits, the example notebooks (including the Stable Baselines3 and RLlib tutorials), the documentation build and the install smoke on macOS and Windows. Before the release, the independent checks of the 3.7.0 audit were run again on the release candidate: 31 runs on `pt_rec_4`, `pt_rec_15`, `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, the three-phase demo and Quebec recomputed the EV physics and departure KPIs, the BAU and the KPI baseline, the household, PV and outage balances and the KPI families from the raw series, and found no mismatch against the documented definitions. The ladder from the BAU to `RBCCommunityPolicy` on `pt_rec_4` and `pt_rec_15` is the same as in 3.7.0. The Gymnasium environment checker passes on the Stable Baselines3 and RLlib wrappers of three datasets; every observation of a full random episode lies inside its space, the last step returns `terminated=False, truncated=True`, and two environments with the same seed give the same observations, random actions and trajectories.

## v3.7.0 - 2026-11-26

### What's new

3.7.0 collects 84 pull requests, most of them from a full audit of 3.6.0 that went through the physics, the KPIs, the baselines, the random streams, the interfaces and the documentation, and from the audit rounds that followed, which checked each fix against an independent recomputation. The physics and the baselines are now versioned, so a run can reproduce 3.6.0 (`physics_version=1`, `baseline_version=1`) while the defaults follow the corrected definitions; the manifest of every export records both settings. The BAU and the rule-based baselines plan from the current step, run the HVAC at the dataset demand and charge the EVs to their targets, and the default KPI baseline adds back the EV charging of the BAU, so on the Portuguese REC datasets the ladder from the BAU to `RBCCommunityPolicy` holds at the same EV service. The KPI reference documents every family and is generated together with a KPI catalogue from one source, the ratios have explicit rules for zero and negligible references, and Mark Colley added opt-in KPIs for outage resilience, recurring evaluation windows, battery and EV battery health and district feeder limits. In addition, the random streams are derived per purpose, object and episode, invalid settings fail at construction, PyTorch and scikit-learn become optional extras, the per-step histories use preallocated buffers, and the documentation gains a quickstart, a complete API and schema reference and one site per release. Results change where a fix applies; the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-360-to-370) lists which runs to repeat and the options that restore the 3.6.0 behaviour.

### Added

- **Per-outage-event resilience metrics (opt-in).** `CityLearnEnv.evaluate_outage_events()` returns `(events_df, summary_df)` and `CityLearnEnv.get_outage_resilience_audit()` returns the structured audit dictionary (including district outage windows in `district_windows`) for static-topology episodes ([#219](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/219)). Built directly on `citylearn/internal/service_energy.py`, each maximal contiguous run of executed steps where `simulate_power_outage` is enabled and `power_outage_signal` is finite and nonzero reports start/end steps, duration in steps and hours, expected/served/unserved energy in kWh with per-end-use splits (`cooling`, `heating`, `dhw`, `non_shiftable`), and `peak_unserved_power_kw` scaled by `seconds_per_time_step`. `summary_df` distinguishes building outage events (`building_events_count`, `building_outage_steps_count`, `building_outage_hours`, `worst_building_event_unserved_energy_kwh`, `fully_served_building_events_ratio`) from district outage windows spanning contiguous steps with at least one affected building (`district_windows_count`, `district_window_steps_count`, `district_window_hours`, `worst_district_window_unserved_energy_kwh`, `fully_served_district_windows_ratio`). `evaluate_v2()` and export outputs are unchanged. See the [KPIs reference](kpis_reference.md#outage-event-resilience-audit).
- **Recurring evaluation-window KPIs.** Opt-in `evaluation_window` configuration (schema key, `CityLearnEnv`, `MultiCommunityEnv` and CLI `-ke`) adds recurring local-window KPIs without changing simulation physics or existing KPI contracts when disabled: `{building,district,portfolio}_energy_grid_window_average_power_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_kw` and `{building,district,portfolio}_ev_window_net_exchange_control_kwh`. Enabling `evaluation_window` without bounds defaults to `16:00:00` to `19:00:00` in scenario local time, supports sub-hour boundaries, straddling steps and midnight wrap (`start > end`), records the resolved bounds and time zone in `exported_kpis.manifest.json`, flags conflicting evaluation windows in `compare_manifests()`, and keeps shared KPIs paired while noting one-sided windowed rows when comparing an enabled run with a disabled run ([#338](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/338)).
- **Battery health and wear properties and EV battery health KPIs.** `Battery` (and `ElectricVehicle.battery`) now exposes read-only `current_state_of_health`, `state_of_health`, `equivalent_full_cycles` and `capacity_fade_kwh` properties, with `equivalent_full_cycles` computed from accepted gross battery-terminal throughput. `CityLearnEnv.evaluate_v2()` and `MultiCommunityEnv.evaluate_v2()` accept `include_battery_health_audit: bool = False` to append EV battery terminal SOH (`*_ev_battery_health_terminal_soh_ratio`), capacity fade (`*_ev_battery_health_capacity_fade_kwh`, `*_ev_battery_health_capacity_fade_ratio`) and equivalent full cycles (`*_ev_battery_health_equivalent_full_cycles_count`), attributing building-level EV wear to realized connections and executed transfers and pooling SOH, fade ratios and equivalent full cycles by nominal capacity at district and portfolio levels; see the [KPI reference](kpis_reference.md#electric-vehicles). Disabled by default, so existing KPI tables are unchanged.
- **District feeder limit exceedance monitoring.** Opt-in `district_electrical_service` configuration (schema key and `CityLearnEnv` argument, with independently optional scalar or dataset-step-0 anchored `import_kw` and `export_kw` limits) measures and reports settled district feeder exceedances without enforcing or curtailing device dispatch, so every simulated quantity remains identical to a run without `district_electrical_service`. Per-step exceedances in `kW` are exposed on `env.feeder_import_exceedance_kw` and `env.feeder_export_exceedance_kw`, `evaluate_v2()` emits `district_energy_grid_feeder_limit_{import,export}_exceedance_energy_total_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_kwh`, `district_energy_grid_feeder_limit_{import,export}_exceedance_peak_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_kw` and `district_energy_grid_feeder_limit_{import,export}_exceedance_step_{control,business_as_usual,delta_to_business_as_usual}_count` when enabled (undefined for an unconfigured direction), and normalized settings are recorded in `effective_schema`, `physical_configuration_sha256`, and `compare_manifests()` ([#334](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/334)).

- **EV SOC carried across episode splits, opt-in `ev_soc_carryover`.** With an episode split, an EV connected across a boundary started the next episode from the schedule SOC, so the charge of the previous episode was lost. On `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` with the BAU from step 1900, 30 × 24-step episodes met 194 departures under `shared` and 163 under `disjoint`, against 213 and 221 in one run over the same rows. With `ev_soc_carryover=True` (constructor or schema key), an episode that continues the previous one in time starts each EV from the SOC it reached at the end of that episode, and the departure rules count a session that crosses the boundary where it really leaves. The 30 × 24 splits then meet 213 (`shared`) and 221 (`disjoint`) departures, and 2 × 100 splits meet 67 under both boundaries, the same as one run. The carried SOC is the session's start SOC for every EV KPI, including feasibility and the energy accounting. Summed over the episodes, departures, met departures and feasibility classes match one run; the connected SOC gain is each episode's own gain, so its sum is lower than in one run. Under `shared` a session reaches the boundary row with the same SOC as in one run, although the controller can act differently at step 0; under `disjoint` the uncontrolled row between two episodes can leave a session up to one charging step behind. A carried episode starts from the SOC the previous controller left, so the same carried episode run by two controllers is not comparable. Sequential splits carry; random and rolling splits never carry, even when a random pick follows the previous episode, and the env warns once that the setting has no effect there; an explicit list carries only when a window starts exactly where the previous one ends for the split boundary, so under the default `shared` a list without a gap does not carry (it does under `disjoint`, or when the next window starts on the previous window's last row). Only the session connected across the boundary is carried, at the first step; later arrivals take the file SOC, or keep the simulated SOC when the file has none, as in one run. Only the EV SOC is carried; batteries, thermal storage and indoor temperatures still reset. Off, episodes stay independent; on, an episode that continues the previous one depends on the previous controller. The BAU reference starts from the same carried SOC, the setting is recorded in `get_metadata()` and the export manifest, and `compare_exports` reports runs with different values as incompatible. The default is off, so results do not change. See the [migration guide](migration.md#ev-soc-carried-across-episode-splits) ([#388](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/388)).
- **HVAC in the BAU.** On datasets with HVAC actions (temperature dynamics), the BAU sent zero to `heating_device`, `cooling_device` and `cooling_or_heating_device`, so its buildings were neither heated nor cooled and every `*_business_as_usual` row compared the controller with unheated or uncooled houses. Under `baseline_version=2`, the default, the BAU now delivers the dataset's own heating and cooling demand at each step, clipped to the action bounds and device limits, and on those datasets its definition becomes `immediate-full-charge-v4+hvac-dataset-demand-v1`. On datasets with HVAC actions the default `*_business_as_usual` rows and the deltas and ratios to the BAU therefore change; on datasets without HVAC actions nothing changes, bit for bit, including the BAU definition (checked over a full episode of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` and `pt_rec_4` with the BAU and the three rule-based baselines). `baseline_version=1` keeps the BAU with the HVAC at zero, as in 3.6.0, and `BusinessAsUsualAgent(env, hvac=...)` overrides the environment for one agent. On Quebec over the first two weeks, the BAU delivers 59,304 kWh of heat instead of 1,725 kWh (the dataset demand is 59,304 kWh), its mean indoor temperature goes from 3.4 °C to 20.6 °C, its district cost from 631.8 to 4,403.8 EUR, and the cost ratio of `RBCSmartPolicy` to the BAU from 6.970 to 1.000. A controller that does not drive the HVAC, such as `ZeroActionAgent`, now has a cost ratio of 0.143 there, so read the ratios to the BAU together with the comfort KPIs. See [HVAC and the baseline version](baselines.md#hvac-and-the-baseline-version) and the [migration guide](migration.md#hvac-in-the-bau) ([#362](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/362)).
- **`evaluate_v2()` table from the CLI.** `citylearn simulate ... evaluate` and `train --evaluate` wrote only the legacy `evaluate()` KPIs, 21 per entity and mostly ratios to the conventional baseline, so CLI runs had no community market rows, no business-as-usual comparison and none of the KPIs in the [KPI reference](kpis_reference.md). They now also write `<simulation_id>-evaluation_v2.csv`, the `evaluate_v2()` table with its market and `*_business_as_usual_*` rows. `--no_business_as_usual` skips the business-as-usual rollout. `<simulation_id>-evaluation.json` keeps its format, legacy KPIs included, so existing parsers are unaffected. Simulation results do not change ([#367](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/367)).
- **RLlib wrappers on the current Ray.** `RLlibSingleAgentWrapper` and `RLlibMultiAgentEnv` are tested with Ray 2.60.0 (new API stack, Python 3.10 to 3.14, gymnasium 1.2.2): PPO and SAC train for a few iterations on one day of `citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation`, and the trained `RLModule` produces actions (`tests/test_rllib_wrappers.py`, skipped without Ray, run in the CI job that installs Ray). `RLlibMultiAgentEnv` failed on that release at the first `reset()` because it set neither `agents` nor `possible_agents`; it now sets both and the per-agent `observation_spaces` and `action_spaces`. `RLlibMultiAgentActionWrapper` orders the actions by agent ID instead of by the order of the dictionary. A new tutorial, [Train a controller with RLlib](https://www.citylearn.net/usage/rllib_training.html), trains single-agent and multi-agent PPO controllers and evaluates them with `evaluate_v2()`; CI executes it. Simulation results are unchanged ([#293](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/293)).
- **KPI units and descriptions.** `evaluate_v2()` and the long multi-community CSV include `unit` and `description` from the KPI catalogue. Wide CSV and Parquet exports keep their existing columns and provide the definitions in their manifest ([#298](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/298)).
- **KPI catalogue and complete KPI reference.** The [KPI catalogue](KPI_V2_TREE.md) lists every name that `evaluate_v2()` emits, grouped into 21 families, with its meaning in English and Portuguese. It is generated from `citylearn/misc/kpi_catalogue.yml`, and a test fails when an emitted name is missing from it. The [KPI reference](kpis_reference.md) now explains every family, including the audits, energy provenance, the community market, comfort and equity. It gives their equations, district and portfolio aggregation and undefined values, and names the canonical discomfort, Gini and local-share KPIs. Values do not change. ([#326](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/326))
- **Control values of the grid-shape KPIs.** With the BAU comparison, `evaluate_v2()` now also emits the control value behind each shape delta: `district_energy_grid_shape_quality_{peak_daily_average,peak_all_time_average,ramping_average,ramping_total}_control_kw` and `district_energy_grid_shape_quality_load_factor_penalty_{daily,monthly}_average{,_signed_net}_control_ratio`. Existing rows do not change. ([#326](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/326))
- **Documentation per release.** The site home (`/`, and `/stable/`) opens the latest final release by default while preserving existing links to shared documentation pages and redirecting development-only root URLs to `/dev/<page>`; each release tag `v*` is published under `/<version>/`, and `master` is published under `/dev/`. Every page provides both a top version selector and an **Other versions** section in the left sidebar driven by `/versions.json`, keeping the equivalent page open when switching versions or falling back to that version's home page when missing ([`docs/publishing.md`](publishing.md#documentation-site), [#296](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/296)).
- **Reward versions.** `Electric_Vehicles_Reward_Function`, `ComfortReward` and `SolarPenaltyAndComfortReward` accept `version`, and version 2 follows their documented definitions ([#249](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/249)). In version 1, a building with EV chargers got only its EV terms, a building without chargers got 0, and leaving 0.25 or more below the required departure SOC added `weights['soc_under'] ** 2`, a bonus of +25 with the default weights; with a required SOC of 0.8, departing at 0.2 scored +15 and departing at 0.65 scored -20. Version 2 adds the MARL base reward to every building and replaces the bonus with `min(2 * w, -(w ** 2))` (-25), so the same departures score -35 and -20. In cooling and heating mode, the version 1 comfort reward took heating or cooling from the larger demand at the step, so in heating mode at 21.5 °C with a 20 °C set point it was 0 with the heater off and -1.5 with it on; version 2 takes it from `hvac_mode` and gives -1.5 in both cases. Version 2 is the default, and every bundled schema that uses these rewards sets `"version": 2`, so the rewards of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, the three-phase demos, the 2023 challenge, Quebec and `baeda_3dem` datasets change; simulations and KPIs do not ([#278](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/278)). Version 1 is deprecated: it exists only to reproduce runs made with CityLearn 3.6.0 or earlier, selecting it raises a `DeprecationWarning`, and it will be removed in a later release. A single current definition avoids comparing policies trained on different rewards. The reward evidence in the environment metadata and in export manifests records the version. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-360-to-370).

### Fixed

- **`citylearn simulate -rs` with the rule-based agents.** The CLI passes `-rs <seed>` to the environment and, as `random_seed`, to the agent. `BusinessAsUsualAgent` (the CLI default), `RBCBasicPolicy`, `RBCSmartPolicy` and `RBCCommunityPolicy` read it as an unknown engine hyperparameter and stopped before the first step with `ValueError: ... got unknown hyperparameters: ['random_seed']`, so a seeded work order of BAU or rule-based runs failed. These agents draw no random numbers and, like every CityLearn agent, take their seed from the environment: they now accept `random_seed` and ignore it. Engine hyperparameters are still checked, and the agents' hyperparameters and comparison identity do not change. A run with `-rs 3` gives the same `evaluate_v2()` table as one seeded with `-ke '{"random_seed": 3}'`. Results unchanged.
- **Dataset cache keyed by content.** The dataset cache folder was named only after the release tag, `v{__version__}`, so every checkout and installation of the same version shared `~/.cache/citylearn/v3.6.0/datasets`. A run by dataset name read whichever copy was cached first, even when the local `data/datasets` had changed: on one development machine, 9 cached `schema.json` files differed from the checkout, such as the `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` reward without `"version": 2`. When another process found a copy incomplete, it moved the folder aside and replaced it while environments were still reading it, and their next `evaluate_v2()` raised `Native BAU inputs changed since loading`. A complete local copy, such as `data/datasets` of a checkout or a folder under `CITYLEARN_LOCAL_DATASETS_PATH`, is now cached in `datasets_by_content/<key>/<dataset>` of the cache folder, where the key is a digest of the paths and bytes of its files. Different files get a different folder, so a checkout never reads another one's copy, and a published copy is never replaced while it is in use; only a damaged one, with input files missing, is repaired. The key is computed once per process and again only when a file's size or modification time changes, so a cache hit costs one `stat` per file (4 ms on `quebec_neighborhood_without_demand_response_set_points`). Downloads, used when there is no local copy, stay under `datasets/<dataset>` of the release tag. Simulation results do not change. See the [migration guide](migration.md#dataset-cache-keyed-by-content).
- **Balance audit tolerance on longer windows.** `IndependentBalanceAudit` compared the district balance residuals, such as `grid_balance_residual_kwh`, with a fixed 1e-5 kWh. A residual is the window's source total minus its destination flows, so its rounding grows with those flows: `scripts/audit/audit_balances.py` on `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo` passed over 168 steps and failed over 336, by 1.2e-5 kWh with the BAU and 1.6e-5 kWh with seeded random actions, with the energy balanced. These checks now add `flow_relative_tolerance` (default 1e-8) times the gross flow of the residual, the sum of the absolute source and destination terms. On that demo the rounding is at most 2e-9 of that flow, so both 336-step runs pass with a margin of at least 5, and a 1e-3 kWh imbalance still fails on any window with less than 99 MWh of gross flow. Other checks and the simulation do not change.
- **Balance audit on large communities.** `IndependentBalanceAudit` slowed the rollout down with the number of members: each interval built the trade graph and each buyer's import mix with Python loops over members × members × labels, checked every node, flow-equation and cohort residual one call at a time, and kept every provenance bag as a dictionary of small arrays. On `tx_travis_county_neighborhood` (100 buildings, BAU, 336 steps) the rollout took 2,225 s against about 40 s without the audit. The trade graph and mixes are now array operations, the checks are vectorised with the same rule, count and first failure, the bags are arrays over a label index, and an acyclic network (the usual case) is solved by LU, with `lstsq` kept for networks with a cycle. Measured single-threaded on the same machine: 48 steps of `tx_travis_county_neighborhood` take 5.1 s with the audit and 3.0 s without it (274.2 s before); 336 steps of `quebec_neighborhood_with_demand_response_set_points` take 5.4 s and 4.8 s (17.6 s before). The audit reports the same results (district provenance within 4e-15 kWh on tx_travis, 3e-11 kWh on the EV and market datasets) and the same maximum error. `checks` is lower because label columns that are zero in every equation are no longer counted. Results of the simulation are unchanged ([#401](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/401)).
- **Flat wrappers on entity-interface datasets.** The `pt_rec_*` datasets default to `interface='entity'`, and `NormalizedObservationWrapper` read the entity dictionaries as flat lists: `reset()` raised `TypeError: can't multiply sequence by non-int of type 'float'` with `central_agent=False` and `KeyError: 0` with `central_agent=True`. The other wrappers failed in the same way on every entity environment. The flat-vector wrappers (`ClippedObservationWrapper`, the normalised, discrete and tabular Q-learning wrappers, the Stable Baselines3 wrappers and the RLlib multi-agent wrappers) now raise a `ValueError` at construction that names `interface='flat'`. With `interface='flat'` the wrappers normalise the `pt_rec_*` observations as on the other datasets. Observations and simulation results do not change ([#351](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/351)).
- **Out-of-range flat actions are clipped to the action space.** Entity `tables` and `map` payloads were clipped to `env.action_space`, but flat actions, and flat vectors sent to an entity environment, reached the devices as given, so only the device limits applied and the same request gave a different simulation depending on the payload. On `citylearn_challenge_2020_climate_zone_1`, random actions at three times the range charged and discharged the thermal storage beyond its bounds: over 48 steps the flat run differed from the clipped run by up to 8.2 kWh per building-step, and district import was 7,346.6 kWh instead of 7,342.0 kWh, while an entity table payload with the same actions gave the clipped result. Every payload now follows one rule: a finite action outside the space is clipped to the nearest bound, an action inside the space is applied as sent, bit for bit, and NaN or infinite actions are still rejected. Physics version 1 keeps the old rule, flat actions applied as given, so runs made with CityLearn 3.6.0 can be reproduced. The hour-based `BasicRBC`, `OptimizedRBC` and `BasicBatteryRBC` apply their storage rule to EV chargers too, so on `pt_rec_4`, whose chargers cannot discharge, they sent negative EV actions (with no effect on the simulation); they now keep their actions inside their action space. Only runs that send actions outside the space change. The BAU, `RBCBasicPolicy`, `RBCSmartPolicy`, `RBCCommunityPolicy`, `ZeroActionAgent` and seeded random actions inside the space give bit-identical series, rewards and `evaluate_v2()` tables on `citylearn_challenge_2020_climate_zone_1`, `citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation`, `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` and `pt_rec_4`, and so do the hour RBCs on the last three; Stable Baselines3 agents already clip or squash their actions to the space. The hour RBCs send a fixed fraction of the storage capacity (for example 0.091 for `BasicRBC`), which is above the thermal storage bound (`nominal_power / capacity`) of some buildings of `baeda_3dem`, the four `citylearn_challenge_2020_climate_zone_*` datasets and `citylearn_challenge_2021`, so their results there can change under the default physics version. Over the default episodes (the first year of `citylearn_challenge_2021`), `BasicBatteryRBC` imports 0.009% less on `citylearn_challenge_2020_climate_zone_4` and 0.004% less on `citylearn_challenge_2021`, and the other 15 affected combinations of dataset and hour RBC do not change; over the first week of `citylearn_challenge_2020_climate_zone_1`, district import is 21,275.0 kWh instead of 21,275.9 kWh with `BasicRBC` and 21,539.9 kWh instead of 21,534.8 kWh with `BasicBatteryRBC`. See [out-of-range actions](actions_reference.md#out-of-range-actions) and the [migration guide](migration.md#out-of-range-flat-actions-are-clipped).
- **Deferrable energy observations bounded by the cycles.** The flat `deferrable_appliance_*_cycle_energy_kwh`, `*_remaining_energy_kwh` and `*_current_step_energy_kwh` observations had the placeholder bounds [-1, 1e6], so `NormalizedObservationWrapper` turned them into constants of about 1e-6: an agent could not see how much energy a cycle needs. They are now bounded by the cycle definitions of the building's appliances: [0, largest cycle energy] for the cycle and remaining energy, and [0, largest step energy] for the current-step energy. No cycle can report more, so the observations stay inside the space under any controller. On `pt_rec_4` (one week, random actions) `deferrable_appliance_DEF-M002-02_remaining_energy_kwh` ranges over 0–1.229 kWh and now normalises to 0–0.585 instead of 1.0e-6–2.2e-6. Physics version 1 keeps the placeholder bounds, so runs made with CityLearn 3.6.0 can be reproduced. The entity `deferrable_appliance` table already had finite bounds derived from the cycle energies and does not change. Only the flat observation space, and what reads it, changes: the simulation, rewards and KPIs for given actions do not (checked bit for bit, see the first item), but normalised and discretised observations and the `SAC`/`MARLISA` encoders do, so policies trained on datasets with deferrable appliances (`pt_rec_*`, `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` and the three-phase demos) see different inputs. See [observation bounds](interfaces_flat_entity.md#observation-bounds) and the [migration guide](migration.md#deferrable-energy-observation-bounds).
- **Occupant set points stay in a plausible range.** `LogisticRegressionOccupantInteractionBuilding` added each occupant change to the current set point with no limit. With `set_point_hold_time_steps` the set point returns to the schedule after 4 steps without interaction, but under arbitrary HVAC control the occupant keeps interacting, so over the first episode of `quebec_neighborhood_with_demand_response_set_points` the heating set point random-walked to -2323 °C with random actions and to 3230 °C with `BusinessAsUsualAgent`, and the observation left its own space ([#357](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/357)). Under physics version 2 an occupant change now stops at `set_point_limits`, a new building schema key that defaults to [15, 30] °C; a schedule value outside the range is not moved into it. The occupant-interaction delta observation still reports the change the occupant asked for. The set-point observation bounds become the schedule range widened to these limits, [15, 30] °C on both Quebec neighbourhoods, instead of the indoor temperature limits. Physics version 1 is unchanged. With `NoControlAgent` the set points stay within 16.6 to 26.6 °C and nothing changes. With `BusinessAsUsualAgent` the simulation and the `evaluate()` KPIs do not change, but the set-point observations and the rewards that read them do. With random actions the comfort KPIs change and the energy KPIs do not. The LSTM indoor temperature is not limited by this change. See the [migration guide](migration.md#occupant-set-point-limits) ([#352](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/352)).
- **EV arrival SOC of 1 % read as 100 %.** Charger files were read value by value: a SOC in [0, 1] was a ratio and one above 1 a percentage, so an arrival SOC of 1 (1 %) in a percent file became 100 % and the EV was not charged. Under physics version 2 each SOC column of a charger file has one unit, decided once from the whole column (percent if any value is above 1), and a charger can declare it with the new `soc_unit` schema key. The charger files of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, the three hourly three-phase demos, and the 15-second `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo_15s_parquet` and `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_assets_only_demo_15s_parquet` contain such rows (8 arrivals in the simulated year of each; `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_asset_changes_demo_15s_parquet` has none), so their results change, those of the two 15-second demos too: over the year of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` the BAU charges 534 kWh more (0.58 %), and district import rises by 0.31 % and cost by 0.33 %; the hourly three-phase demos change by 0.31 to 0.38 %. `pt_rec_*` is unchanged. A warning flags a column read as percent that also holds values between 0 and 1, as one stray value above 1 in a ratio column would cause, and a misspelt charger key such as `soc_units` is reported. Physics version 1 keeps the old rule. See the [migration guide](migration.md#ev-soc-unit-of-charger-files) ([#368](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/368)).
- **Charger defaults and misspelt attributes.** A charger without `max_discharging_power` was a 50 kW V2G charger, and one without `max_charging_power` or `efficiency` charged at 50 kW with efficiency 1.0; misspelt attribute names were dropped without notice, and the bundled `nominal_power` and `charger_type` keys did nothing. Unknown charger attributes now raise a `ValueError` with a suggestion. Under physics version 2 an omitted `max_discharging_power` is 0 (no V2G) and an omitted `max_charging_power` is `nominal_power`; a warning names every limit that falls back to an assumed value (`efficiency` only when an efficiency curve does not replace it). `charger_type` is kept as a label. Every bundled charger sets its limits, so bundled results do not change. Physics version 1 keeps the 50 kW defaults. See the [migration guide](migration.md#charger-defaults-and-attribute-names) ([#369](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/369)).
- **Duplicate charger ids are rejected.** Two buildings of a schema could declare a charger with the same id: the env loaded with only a double-booking warning such as `Charger 'charger_1_1' ... already plugged into charger 'charger_1_1'`, and the id no longer named one charger in observation and action names, EV sessions, export file names and raw entity-map keys. Loading now raises a `ValueError` that names the id and both buildings, in static and dynamic topology. Chargers cloned by topology events already get their own ids ([#394](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/394)), and no bundled dataset repeats a charger id, so results are unchanged. Deferrable-appliance names stay scoped to their building (the bundled dynamic-topology demo has `deferrable_appliance_1` in two buildings), but an entity action map that uses such a name without its building now raises instead of applying the action to the last building with that name ([#402](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/402)).
- **EV departures at the end of an episode are counted once.** The KPI reference counts a departure at the last step when the EV is still connected and its countdown to departure is in [0, 1], but the code tested the schedule's terminal row, which the KPI loop never reaches (an episode of N rows has N − 1 control steps). That session was neither counted in `ev_events`, `ev_performance` and `ev_energy_accounting_connected_soc_gain_kwh` nor in the departure audit. The rule now applies at the last step of the member's KPI window. With split episodes, the next episode used to count such a session at its step 0 with the SOC set at reset or, under `episode_split_boundary='disjoint'`, not at all, and under `'disjoint'` it scored a session already connected on the row between the episodes at step 0 with the SOC set at reset. The last step of an episode that another `'disjoint'` split follows now also counts a session with countdown 2, which leaves before that split's first control step, and step 0 skips any session that was connected on the row before it, so each session is counted once, with the SOC the controller reached. Over the 100 steps 1900–1999 of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` with the BAU, Building_1, Building_4 and Building_15 each gain one departure, so the district goes from 32 to 35 departures and its success ratio from 0.844 to 0.857. Over two 100-step episodes from step 1900, one run counts 72 departures, 67 of them met. The two episodes now count 35 + 37 under `shared` (32 + 40 before), with 64 met (61 before), because the last step scores a session one charging step before it leaves; under `disjoint` they count 40 + 32 (32 + 37 before), with 67 met (59 before), the same as one run. The default episodes of the bundled EV datasets do not end in such a session, so their KPIs do not change (checked by simulation on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, the hourly three-phase demos and `pt_rec_4`, and on the charger schedules of the other `pt_rec_*` and the 15-second demos). Simulations do not change. See the [migration guide](migration.md#ev-departures-at-the-end-of-the-window) ([#370](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/370)).
- **Multiple storage transfers in one step.** Calling `Battery.charge()` (or `StorageDevice.charge()`) more than once at the same `time_step` used to overwrite `energy_balance[t]` with the last transfer while `electricity_consumption[t]` summed every transfer, and appended an extra `capacity_history` entry for each call after the first. `StorageDevice._charge()` and `Battery.charge()` now accumulate `energy_balance[t]` across same-step transfers, add only the incremental transfer to `electricity_consumption[t]`, and update the current step's `capacity_history` entry in place with each transfer's capacity loss. The fix applies to both physics versions 1 and 2; standard simulator dispatch makes at most one transfer per step, so bundled datasets and single-transfer runs are bit-identical under both versions.
- **One power budget per step for repeated storage transfers.** Each `Battery.charge()` call read `get_max_input_power()` or `get_max_output_power()` again and got the full nominal power for the step, so several calls in one step could move several times the energy the battery can deliver: three 5 kWh charges put 13.38 kWh through a 5 kW battery in one hour. `StorageTank.charge()` had the same per-call limit on `max_input_power` and `max_output_power`. Both now give one budget per step on terminal energy, the energy at the device's terminals before conversion losses, and a later call gets only what the earlier calls left. A charge and a discharge in the same step share it: each transfer takes the share of the step its energy is of the power limit in its direction, so |E_in|/P_in + |E_out|/P_out stays within the step duration, and +5 then −5 kWh on a 5 kW battery no longer moves 10 kWh in one hour. For the battery, the budget is read from the power curves at the stored energy before the step's first transfer, and `preview_charge()` applies the same remaining budget. Capacity and depth-of-discharge limits still use the current stored energy. The limit is on terminal energy, so a transfer split into several calls is held to the same terminal energy as one call, but with a sloped `power_efficiency_curve` its stored energy can differ slightly, because each call is converted at its own power. `CityLearnEnv.step()` makes one transfer per device and step, so simulated results and KPIs do not change; only code that calls `charge()` more than once in a step sees the limit ([#363](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/363)).
- **District emissions with local trading off.** With a community market enabled and `local_trading_enabled: false`, the settlement, the cost KPIs and the provenance ledger trade no energy locally, but the district emissions still netted one member's export against another's import. They now charge every member import at its carbon intensity, the `member_sum` value, and equal `district_energy_provenance_external_purchase_emissions_kgco2` in every market configuration. The local price ratio never affects emissions. Results change only for runs with local trading switched off; no bundled dataset does that. See the [migration guide](migration.md#district-emissions-with-local-trading-off) and [#327](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/327).
- **Invalid community market settings fail at construction.** The `community_market` section was read leniently: an `enable` typo left the market off, a ratio that was not a number (`"0,7"`) became 0.8, a ratio above 1 was clipped to 1, `grid_export_price` was ignored unless `settlement_version` was the number 2 (the string `"2"` did not count), and weights for unknown buildings or with invalid values were dropped. Each of these now raises a `ValueError` that names the key, also with `enabled: false`. The ratio must be a number from 0 to 1 (a value above 1 raises instead of being clipped, because a member cannot pay more for local energy than for grid energy), the two ratio keys must agree when both are set, `settlement_version` must be the number 1 or 2 (`2.0` still selects version 2), a non-zero `grid_export_price` needs `"settlement_version": 2`, and member weights and tariff files must name buildings of the schema. `evaluate_v2()` with the BAU comparison also raises when market attributes were changed on the env after construction, since the native reference reads the schema and would settle a different market. - **Portfolio market rows with and without markets.** In a `MultiCommunityEnv` that mixes communities with and without a community market, the portfolio community-market rows summed the zeros that a community without a market reports, so they covered only the market communities while every other portfolio row covered all of them. With two copies of `pt_rec_4` over 96 steps, one without a market, the portfolio settled cost was 3.724 EUR against a portfolio cost of 7.449 EUR, and the grid import after local trading was 27.449 kWh against 54.899 kWh imported. A community without a market now enters these portfolio rows with its plain grid settlement: no local trade, its members' grid import and export, its control cost as settled and counterfactual cost, and zero savings and operator balance. The same portfolio now reports 7.449 EUR settled and 54.903 kWh grid import after local trading (the members' own imports, before netting), and its shares of local trade are shares of the whole portfolio. KPI names do not change. District rows, simulations and portfolios where every community has a market, or none has, do not change. See the [migration guide](migration.md#portfolio-market-rows-with-and-without-markets) and [#373](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/373).
- **Unknown keys in a community entry.** `MultiCommunityEnv` read only the keys it knew in each community entry, so a misspelt key fell back to its default without notice: `"wieght": 3.0` left the community at weight 1.0, and `"env_kwarg"` ran the child with the schema defaults. A boolean weight was also read as 1.0 or 0.0. An entry with a key other than `community_id`, `schema`, `env_kwargs` and `weight` now raises a `ValueError` that names the key and lists the accepted ones, and a boolean weight raises a `ValueError`. Valid configurations and their results do not change ([#374](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/374)).
- **Observation spaces describe the observations.** The flat observation space followed `active_observations`, while the observation vector and `observation_names` put each building's EV charger and deferrable appliance features after its other features, so a position could carry the bounds of another feature: on `pt_rec_4`, `Member_001`, position 25 is `charging_building_headroom_kw` (6.8 kW) and had the bounds of an EV state of charge, [-0.1, 1.0]. The space now follows the vector, name by name (`Building.flat_observation_order`); the observation vector and its names do not change. The entity space now declares `meta` (an `EntityMetaSpace`), so `observation_space.contains(observations)` holds. Three estimated bounds could be exceeded and are widened: per-phase charging headroom, which assumed the phase carried the whole building load; cooling and heating demand of dynamics buildings whose HVAC action sets the demand, which can exceed the dataset demand up to the device output at nominal power; and the set points of occupant-interaction buildings, which occupants move step by step outside the schedule. A test runs an episode on five bundled datasets, flat and entity, central and decentralised, and checks every observation against its space. Simulations and KPIs do not change, except for robustness faults on the flat interface of these datasets, which now reach the feature they name instead of the one at its old position. What bound-based normalisation feeds an agent does change, so agents trained on the affected datasets should be retrained. See the [migration guide](migration.md#observation-spaces-follow-the-observations) ([#306](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/306)).
- **One-step delay in the BAU battery response.** The native reference uses current observed load and PV when dispatching the stationary battery. BAU-relative results can change, and the export manifest records the new definition. - **Rule-based baselines that cost more than the BAU.** On `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all`, `RBCBasicPolicy` cost 4% more than the BAU and `RBCSmartPolicy` and `RBCCommunityPolicy` 14% more. There were two causes. First, the three planned their battery, EV and appliance budget from the settled load, PV, battery and EV power of the previous step, while the BAU has planned from the step being planned since #190. On hourly data the battery answered an hour late: it missed the start of the evening peak and discharged against the last hour's import when PV already covered the load. They now use the BAU's current-step estimate. Second, Smart and Community charge the battery from the grid at a cheap price only when no local PV surplus is forecast, but that forecast exists only with the `entity_forecasts_derived` bundle. Without it the expected surplus read as zero. The 2022 block tariff makes 4–8 h cheap against its forecasts every day, so the batteries filled from the grid before sunrise and then exported the PV they could no longer store. Without a surplus forecast, a building with PV now does not charge from the grid at a cheap price while its PV or irradiance forecast shows sun. The definitions become `basic-v4`, `smart-local-v4` and `community-v4`; the BAU does not change. Cost before → after (`district_cost_total_control_eur`; market-settled cost with the community market): `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all`, steps 0–720, Basic 2,937.3 → 2,825.0 EUR (the BAU, bit for bit) and Smart and Community 3,219.3 → 2,812.2 (BAU 2,825.0); the full year, Basic 28,020.3 → 26,136.5 (the BAU) and Smart and Community 30,973.6 → 26,443.5; `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, 30 days, Basic 2,430.4 → 2,364.1 and Smart and Community 2,342.7 → 2,245.0; `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo`, 30 days, Basic 2,180.9 → 2,217.8, Smart 2,085.8 → 2,053.9 and Community 2,082.9 → 2,052.3; the first week of `pt_rec_4` and `pt_rec_15`, changes below 1.1%. EV departures met, appliance service and phase violations (none) do not change in any of these runs. The grid-charge change acts only without `entity_forecasts_derived`: on `pt_rec_*` and `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` every KPI is the same with and without it. See the [migration guide](migration.md#the-rule-based-baselines-use-current-step-load-and-solar) ([#400](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/400)).
- **Rule-based baselines overcharged EVs in the last interval.** In the last interval before departure, `RBCBasicPolicy`, `RBCSmartPolicy` and `RBCCommunityPolicy` requested full charging power to cover the charger and battery losses, which on hourly data with 22 kW chargers put far more energy into the car than the gap: on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` (steps 0–719, seed 0) only 140 of 226 departures ended within 0.05 SOC of the target. The last interval now allows 25% more AC energy than the battery-side gap (`EV_LAST_INTERVAL_LOSS_ALLOWANCE`), and at least the charger's minimum power. The departures met do not change (223 of 226), 219 now end within the tolerance, and the cost goes down: Basic 2,364.06 → 2,279.86 EUR and Smart and Community 2,245.02 → 2,187.05; on `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo` (30 days) Basic 2,217.84 → 2,148.39, Smart 2,053.85 → 2,007.66 and Community 2,052.34 → 2,006.47, with 221 of 226 departures met before and after. The first week of `pt_rec_4` and `pt_rec_15` does not change. On plus_evs, Basic still costs 21 EUR more than the user-target BAU: its fixed price thresholds come from the Portuguese tariff, so every hour of the 2022 tariff reads as expensive, Basic charges only what each departure needs, and that later charging costs 0.161 against 0.154 per kWh for charging on arrival. The definitions keep `basic-v4`, `smart-local-v4` and `community-v4`, introduced in this release.
- **The user-target BAU stopped short of the target.** With `business_as_usual_ev_charge_target='requested'`, the BAU limited its last command to the remaining energy gap and sent nothing when that command was below the charger's minimum power, so on hourly data with 22 kW chargers (3.7 kW minimum) the car left up to a few percent below its target. On `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, steps 0–719, seed 0, it met 173 of 226 departures, against 223 for the full BAU and the rule-based baselines. The last step now charges at the minimum power and crosses the target slightly, as the rule-based baselines do: 223 of 226 are met, the EV charge goes from 4,901 to 5,045 kWh and the cost from 2,240.85 to 2,258.49 EUR; on `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo` (30 days) 169 → 219 met and 2,096.19 → 2,112.86 EUR. On `pt_rec_*` (15 min, 1.38 kW minimum) every departure was already met. The default `'full'` BAU does not change.
- **PV autosizing on a short window.** Under physics version 2, `Building.autosize_pv` compared the demand of the simulation window with a full year of PVWatts output, so a shorter window gave a smaller array: on a one-building copy of `tx_travis_county_neighborhood`, 14.875 kW for a year, 1.275 kW for a month and 0.425 kW for a week. The window's demand is now scaled to one year. A one-year window is unchanged; physics version 1 is unchanged.
- **Flat headroom observations during outages.** With `simulate_power_outage`, `charging_building_headroom_kw` and `charging_building_export_headroom_kw` left their observation space during an outage (6.9 kW against a high bound of 6.8555 kW on `pt_rec_4` Member_002): the bounds assumed the base load never drops below the minimum of the non-shiftable series, but an outage takes it to zero. The bounds now include zero base power when outages are simulated, as the phase bounds already did. Runs without outages are unchanged.

Flat runs are unchanged ([#250](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/250)).
- **`NoControlAgent` with the BAU comparison and a dynamic topology, and hour-based RBCs with deferrable appliances.** `NoControlAgent` disables every action of the env, so `evaluate_v2()` with the native BAU reference raised `ValueError: Native BAU configuration differs ... active_actions` on every dataset; the reference keeps the loaded actions, and the agent now records them for the check. Under a dynamic topology, reset and joining members or assets enable their actions again, and `step()` failed because the agent returned no actions; the agent now disables them again before each action. `BasicRBC`, `OptimizedRBC` and `BasicBatteryRBC` raised `ValueError: Unknown action name: deferrable_appliance_...` on datasets with deferrable appliances (`citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, the `pt_rec_*` datasets); they now start each pending cycle as soon as its window allows, as `BasicElectricVehicleRBC_ReferenceController` does, since a cycle that is never started is missed. - **Rule baselines as SACRBC and MARLISARBC exploration controllers.** `rbc` accepts any agent instance, agent class or import path, including `RBCBasicPolicy`, `RBCSmartPolicy`, `RBCCommunityPolicy` and `BusinessAsUsualAgent`, which raised `TypeError` as instances and `ValueError` for the SAC hyperparameters as classes. - **Physics invariant identities independent of the net balance.** The net-balance check rebuilds net consumption from the device series that produced it, so an accounting error inside a device model passed it: on `tests/data/minute_ev_demo`, 100 kWh added to the consumption of a 1.0 kWh non-shiftable load raised nothing. `physics_invariant_checks` now also checks identities that compare each device series with the model's inputs and states. The electricity of each heat pump and electric heater must equal its heat output, including storage charging, divided by its COP or efficiency. Served cooling, heating, DHW and non-shiftable load must not exceed their demand. A battery's stored energy must change by its terminal energy at its efficiency, and a discharge must stop at the depth-of-discharge reserve. Each identity raises `PhysicsInvariantError`. - **A removed battery keeps its past in the building's series.** In dynamic topology, `remove_asset` for `electrical_storage` replaced the battery with an empty one, and `add_asset` on a building that already had a battery replaced it with the new one. The building's battery series read only the current instance, so for every step the replaced battery had been installed `electrical_storage_electricity_consumption`, `energy_to_electrical_storage` and `energy_from_electrical_storage` read zero, and `net_electricity_consumption_without_storage`, its variants and the district sums kept that battery's dispatch: the default KPI baseline then depended on what the controller did with the battery. Under the default `baseline_version=2`, what the replaced battery moved before the event now stays in the building's series, as removed PV already keeps its past production and a rejoining member its battery history; `baseline_version=1` keeps the 3.6.0 series and results (see the [migration guide](migration.md#dynamic-topology-joining-members-storage-and-district-series)). The battery KPIs, which read each battery's own ledger, the physics and the controlled net consumption do not change. Results change only under `baseline_version=2` in dynamic-topology runs that remove or replace a battery: the `*_baseline_*` rows and the ratios and deltas to the baseline of that building, of the district and, with a community market, the baseline costs of the other members. On `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_topology_demo` (steps 950 to 2150, the bundled event removes `Building_12`'s battery at 1900), the `Building_12` baseline import goes from 1109.0 kWh with the BAU and 1829.1 kWh with seeded random actions to 1181.9 kWh with both, and its baseline export from 26.2 and 648.2 kWh to 115.6 kWh. The district baseline import moves by +24.4 and −123.1 kWh (+0.1 % and −0.5 %). In the `pt_rec_30` topology scenario the `Member_001` battery replacement at step 12840 moves that member's baseline import from 23.4 to 30.4 kWh over steps 12700 to 13200. The entity storage action feedback that looks back over the building's battery series (`time_since_last_nonzero_action_hours`, and `applied_energy_prev_15m_kwh` at steps shorter than 15 minutes) now also sees the replaced battery's last actions, as it already did for a rejoining member: with seeded random actions this changes that observation at the event step only (for example −1 to 40 h for `Member_001` in `pt_rec_30`), and rewards do not change. In `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_asset_changes_demo_15s_parquet`, which removes `Building_9`'s battery at step 11520, its baseline import over steps 11280 to 11760 goes from 2.16 to 1.50 kWh with seeded random actions; with the BAU that battery had not moved energy there. `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_assets_only_demo` adds a battery only where there was none and does not change.
- **Cloned members' chargers have their own ids.** When an `add_member` event cloned a member from `source_member_id`, the clone's chargers kept the source's ids, so the double-booking warning read "already plugged into charger 'charger_1_1'; 'charger_1_1' stays idle", and the clone's observation and action names, entity ids and per-charger export files used the source's id. The clone's chargers are now named after the clone, following the dataset's naming: the source member's name in the id, or else the identifier that ends it, becomes the target's (`charger_1_1` of `Building_1` becomes `charger_19_1` in `Building_19`, `CHG-M001-01` of `Member_001` becomes `CHG-M009-01` in `Member_009`); otherwise `_<target member>` is appended. **Names change for cloned members only:** the clone's `electric_vehicle_storage_<id>` action, its charger, connected-EV, incoming-EV and phase one-hot observations, its entity ids (`Building_19/charger_19_1`) and its `exported_data_<member>_<charger>_ep<n>.csv` file use the new id, and a robustness event that targets the clone's charger must use it. No bundled dataset clones a member, and simulated values do not change.
- **Portable schema hash in the export manifest.** `schema_sha256` in `exported_kpis.manifest.json` hashed the effective schema with its absolute `root_directory`, so the same scenario and data in two folders or on two machines gave two hashes, and the scenario file itself was not hashed. `schema_sha256` now leaves out `root_directory` and counts absolute paths inside the dataset folder as relative to it, so it changes only with the schema content. The manifest also records `schema_file`, the scenario file relative to the dataset folder, and `schema_file_sha256`, the SHA-256 of its bytes (both `None` for an env built from a dictionary). `data_files_sha256` and `compare_manifests()`, which does not read `schema_sha256`, are unchanged. Hashes recorded before this change differ from new ones for the same scenario; see the [migration guide](migration.md#portable-schema-hash). Simulation results do not change.

### Changed

- **Non-finite power-outage flags.** When `simulate_power_outage` is enabled, `Building.reset_power_outage_signal()` now rejects non-finite outage flags (blank cells, `NaN`, `inf`, `-inf`) with a `ValueError` before simulation; when `simulate_power_outage` is disabled, the `power_outage` column is ignored. `Building.power_outage`, `Building._without_grid_during_outage()`, `service_energy.unserved_energy()`, `CostFunction.normalized_unserved_energy()` and the outage resilience audit share the same finite non-zero rule (`Building.power_outage` previously treated `NaN` as an outage). Replace blank or infinite cells in `power_outage` with `0` for grid-connected steps or a finite non-zero value for outages ([#219](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/219)).
- **The rule-based baselines run the HVAC.** `RBCBasicPolicy`, `RBCSmartPolicy` and `RBCCommunityPolicy` sent zero to `heating_device`, `cooling_device` and `cooling_or_heating_device`, so on datasets with HVAC actions their houses were neither heated nor cooled. They now deliver the dataset's own heating and cooling demand at each step, with the rule of the default BAU: a thermostat that meets the load, without optimisation. The storage, EV and appliance rules do not change; on datasets with HVAC actions and batteries the battery actions can change, because the load they react to now includes the HVAC. `SACRBC` and `MARLISARBC` with a rule baseline as `rbc` now explore with the HVAC at the dataset demand; pass `hvac='off'` for the old exploration. `hvac='off'` keeps the zero HVAC. On datasets without HVAC actions the results and `comparison_identity()` are bit-identical to before (checked on the 22 bundled datasets without HVAC actions); on datasets with HVAC actions the identity records `hvac='dataset_demand'` and the suffix `+hvac-dataset-demand-v1`. On Quebec over two weeks the mean indoor temperature goes from 3.4 °C to 20.6 °C and `district_comfort_resilience_discomfort_overall_ratio` from 0.183 to 0.026; the cost goes from 631.8 to 4,403.8 EUR, a cost ratio of 1.000 to the default BAU, which heats the same way, and 6.970 to the `baseline_version=1` BAU, which does not heat. See [HVAC of the rule-based baselines](baselines.md#hvac-of-the-rule-based-baselines) and the [migration guide](migration.md#the-rule-based-baselines-run-the-hvac).
- **Observation bounds documented.** The environment does not clip observations to `observation_space`, and `NormalizedObservationWrapper` outputs can leave [0, 1]; `ClippedObservationWrapper` clips them. The [observation bounds](interfaces_flat_entity.md#observation-bounds) section says how the thermal bounds are estimated and when the indoor temperature leaves them: on the Quebec neighbourhoods, when the HVAC is not run (the default BAU, `ZeroActionAgent`, the rule-based baselines with `hvac='off'`) it falls below `low` in 38% of building-steps and the heating delta in 89%, while with the HVAC at the dataset demand everything stays inside. Simulations, KPIs and rewards do not change ([#357](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/357)).
- **Documentation corrections.** The entity action-map examples in the flat/entity interface page and the actions reference used ids such as `charger:Building_1:AC001`, which `step()` rejects with `Unknown charger id`; they now use the real `<building>/<device>` ids (`charger:Building_1/charger_1_1`) and say where to read them. The multi-community synchronization table is no longer split by a blank line, and the grid section of the KPI reference is whole again, with one portfolio paragraph. [Comparing two exported runs](running_simulations.md#comparing-two-exported-runs) documents `set_experiment_identity()`, without which `compare_exports()` reports two runs as `status='unknown'`. Tests run the documented examples. Simulations and KPIs do not change.
- **Agents renamed: `NoControlAgent` and `ZeroActionAgent`.** In CityLearn, "baseline" names the KPI counterfactual, the same buildings without storage and with PV, which the `*_baseline_*` KPIs report. Two agent classes used the word for something else, so they are renamed after what they do: `citylearn.agents.base.BaselineAgent`, which disables every active action, becomes `NoControlAgent`, and `citylearn.agents.baseline.ZeroActionBaselineAgent`, which requests zero for every action, becomes `ZeroActionAgent` ([#297](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/297)). The old names remain deprecated aliases of the same classes until a later release, so imports, CLI and schema agent paths and pickled agents keep working, with a `DeprecationWarning` that names the new class and the reason. Results are unchanged. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-360-to-370).
- **Lint and CI (engineering, no result change).** `ruff` runs with the rules in `pyproject.toml`: F401 (unused imports removed while keeping explicit `as` re-exports on public modules), F841 (unused variables), B006 (the mutable `hidden_dim` and `hidden_size` defaults in `citylearn/rl.py`, now `None`), E722 (bare `except`) and the earlier E9, F821 and NPY201. Third-party GitHub Actions are pinned to commit SHAs and Dependabot proposes weekly updates. A separate workflow (`.github/workflows/coverage.yml`) reports test coverage (`pytest-cov`, not a gate) outside pull-request checks, and `pytest.ini` sets a 600 s per-test timeout (`pytest-timeout`). Simulation results are unchanged. Switching to trusted publishing remains a maintainer action ([#289](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/289)).
- **Runtime audits in parallel CI jobs (engineering, no result change).** The physics audit, the official dataset audit, and the entity contract and refactor parity audits run as three parallel jobs instead of one after the other. Each stays strict, uploads its own JSON evidence (`citylearn-runtime-audits-<name>`) and shows by its job name which audit failed; the `runtime-audits` job fails unless all three pass ([#339](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/339)).
- **Cheaper physics invariant checks (no result change).** The `pt_rec_*` schemas turn on `physics_invariant_checks`, and since 3.6.0 the checks also compare each heat pump and electric heater with its heat output, the served energy with the demand, and each battery's stored energy with its terminal energy. They cost about 1.4 ms per step on `pt_rec_15` and 2.9 ms on `pt_rec_30`, 15 % of the step time, against 0.36 and 0.75 ms in 3.6.0. The battery identity also copied the battery's whole efficiency history, one entry per step, at every step, so its cost grew with the episode. The checks now read only the efficiency entries of the step's operations, format their messages only for a violation, read the thermal end uses once per building and step, and test finiteness with `math.isfinite`. Every check, tolerance and message is the same: on 736,608 perturbed states over seven datasets (pt_rec_4, pt_rec_15, the 2021 challenge, plus_evs, the 2023 phase 3, the hourly three-phase demo and the minute EV test schema), the earlier and the new checks raise the same 540,051 violations with the same messages and pass the rest. Over 960 steps with zero actions under the flat interface (median of three interleaved runs on a shared machine), the checks take 0.60 s instead of 1.34 s on `pt_rec_15` (3.6.0: 0.34 s) and 1.26 s instead of 2.76 s on `pt_rec_30` (3.6.0: 0.72 s); over a month of `pt_rec_15` (2,976 steps) 1.9 s instead of 4.6 s. A `pt_rec_15` rollout runs at 118 instead of 106 steps per second (126 with the checks off), `pt_rec_30` at 58 instead of 53. Observations, rewards and the `evaluate_v2()` table are bit-identical under both interfaces.
- **Faster construction on the 15 s datasets (no result change).** The SOC unit of each charger file (#368) is decided from every row of the file, and each SOC column was converted to Python objects and through `pd.to_numeric`, twice. On `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo_15s_parquet` (2.1 M rows, 8 chargers) that took about 4 s, and the business-as-usual reference of `evaluate_v2()` paid it again. Numeric columns are now converted directly; other values keep the `pd.to_numeric` path. Building that environment over one day (5,760 steps) takes 0.9 to 1.4 s instead of 4.1 to 6.0 s, `evaluate_v2()` after 48 steps 2.7 s instead of 7.3 s, and peak memory falls from about 360 to 265 MB; `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` over a year builds in 0.54 to 0.61 s instead of 0.62 to 0.82 s. The detected units, the warning for fractions in a percent column, the charger data and the `evaluate_v2()` table are unchanged.
- **Faster occupant set-point model (no result change).** The occupant forests are evaluated one input row at a time, and each prediction walked their 100 to 300 trees one after the other, about 10 to 16 ms per prediction. With the HVAC off (the `baseline_version=1` BAU, random actions, or any controller that leaves the HVAC idle) the cold Quebec buildings ask the model about 40 times more often, so these runs were 3 to 6 times slower than with the HVAC on. All trees of a forest are now walked together, one level per iteration, with the same comparisons, missing-value routes, leaf values and order of the sum over trees, so predictions are bit-identical; one prediction takes about 0.1 ms. Over two weeks of `quebec_neighborhood_without_demand_response_set_points`, a rollout plus `evaluate_v2()` takes 17 to 22 s instead of 134 to 158 s with the BAU without HVAC (now `baseline_version=1`), 25 to 32 s instead of 119 to 144 s with random actions, and 17 to 20 s instead of 27 s with the BAU that runs the HVAC (now the default `baseline_version=2`) (two interleaved runs each, on a shared machine). Observations, actions, every building series and the `evaluate_v2()` table are bit-identical to before on Quebec under the BAU with and without HVAC, `RBCSmartPolicy` and random actions.

- **Logging and NumPy state.** Importing CityLearn no longer calls `logging.basicConfig` or `np.seterr`, and the package logs through `citylearn.*` loggers, so it no longer changes the host program's root logger or NumPy error handling. Warnings still reach stderr; configure logging in your application (for example `logging.basicConfig(level=logging.INFO)`) to see INFO messages. The CLI does this itself, and `DataSet`/`Agent.learn()` change the `citylearn` logger's level only when `logging_level` is passed ([#251](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/251)).
- **Export folder.** The default render folder and relative `render_directory` values resolve against the current working directory (`./render_logs`); they used to resolve against the package's parent folder, which is `site-packages` for an installed wheel ([#254](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/254)).
- **LSTM checkpoints** load with `torch.load(..., weights_only=True)` on the CPU, so with torch 1.13 or newer a checkpoint shipped with a dataset cannot run code when unpickled, and a malformed checkpoint gives its real error ([#255](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/255)).
- **Preallocated per-step histories.** `env.rewards` and the district `env.net_electricity_consumption`, `env.net_electricity_consumption_cost` and `env.net_electricity_consumption_emission` are kept in float64 buffers sized for the episode instead of growing Python lists. The attributes return `_TrackedList`, a `list` subclass (`isinstance(..., list)` is `True` and `type(...) is list` is `False`, pickling as a plain `list`), with the same element types (`float` rows for `rewards`, `np.float64` for the district series) and one entry per executed step; the public list is materialized lazily on first access, extended incrementally when the property is read again so existing `[-1]` monitoring loops stay constant-time per step, and detached before any in-place mutation so editing a returned list never alters stored values or subsequent reads. A reference kept across `env.step()` calls stays at the length it had when read until the property is read again, so callers should re-read the property on `env` for updated histories. Read the latest step value with `env.rewards[-1]`, `env.net_electricity_consumption[-1]`, `env.net_electricity_consumption_cost[-1]` or `env.net_electricity_consumption_emission[-1]`; each read takes constant time per step. Once read, the list is kept for the rest of the episode, so the memory saving holds only while nothing reads these properties during the episode; training loops should use the reward returned by `env.step()`, as the CityLearn agents do. Rewards or series that a buffer cannot reproduce (custom list subclasses, non-list rows, other element types) move to plain lists built from the authoritative buffer values while preserving row types and defensive copies. CLI evaluation JSON export streams `episode_rewards` row by row without building the full Python list. With 17 buildings on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` (flat, zero actions, 500 steps after 100 warm-up steps), tracemalloc growth fell from 1640 to 862 B per step (rewards 655 B to 0, district series about 120 B to 0), and the buffers reserve about 170 B per step of the episode up front. Step time is unchanged (3.47 ms against 3.45 ms, median of 5 runs). Rewards, observations, `evaluate_v2()` tables, exports and the CLI JSON are bit-identical to before on the flat and entity interfaces, an LSTM dataset, a central-agent run, multi-episode runs and a dynamic-topology run. The battery `efficiency_history` and the provenance event records keep growing as before ([#290](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/290)).

Simulation results and KPIs of valid runs are unchanged by these items: the BAU and `RBCSmartPolicy` give bit-identical actions and `evaluate_v2()` tables to 3.6.0 over a week of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` and of `pt_rec_30` with `baseline_version=1`. With the default `baseline_version=2`, the baseline-derived KPIs of these EV datasets change (see "EV charging in the default baseline" under KPI definitions). See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-360-to-370).

### Dynamic topology and data alignment

Work package 3 of the 3.6.0 audit ([#226](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/226)), in [#274](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/274). Results change for dynamic-topology runs with member-window files and for consecutive episode splits; the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-360-to-370) lists the options that restore the 3.6.0 behaviour.

- **Consecutive episode splits share their boundary row.** Consecutive splits used to advance by `episode_time_steps` rows, so the interval between two episodes was never simulated. They now advance by `episode_time_steps - 1` rows, and every interval up to the last complete split is simulated once; `episode_split_boundary='disjoint'` keeps the 3.6.0 splits. ([#247](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/247))
- **Dynamic-topology members are aligned with the simulation window.** A member file shorter than the dataset is read as starting at the member's `add_member` time step for any `simulation_start_time_step` and window length, which also corrects the native BAU inputs of dynamic episodes that do not start at row 0. A member that rejoins needs a full-horizon file, members that join mid-episode start their thermal storage at `initial_soc`, and the public district series keep departed members and count joining members only from their activation. ([#246](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/246))
- **A topology event at the terminal step** no longer adds an unexecuted interval to the building series.
- **A control step different from the dataset step is rejected.** CityLearn reads one data row per control step and does not resample, so such a run advanced the calendar, prices and EV schedules at the wrong rate. It now raises a `ValueError`; `time_step_ratio_mode='compress'` keeps the 3.6.0 behaviour. The dataset step is inferred from every row instead of the first two, and the `time_step_ratio` argument must match it. Bundled datasets are not affected. ([#234](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/234))

### KPI definitions

These changes alter KPI values, not simulations. The KPI definition version becomes `2.0.0`; the [migration guide](migration.md#kpi-definitions) lists what to rerun, and the [KPI reference](kpis_reference.md#definitions-changed-after-360) lists every changed row and the name that keeps its 3.6.0 value.

- **EV charging in the default baseline.** The default `baseline` (`WITHOUT_STORAGE_BUT_WITH_PV`) subtracted the EV charger consumption together with the battery and thermal storage and added nothing back, so on EV datasets every `*_baseline_*`, `*_delta_*` and `*_ratio_to_baseline_*` row, and the whole legacy `evaluate()` table, compared the run with a community whose cars never charge; the docstring said the opposite. The baseline now adds back the EV charging of the native BAU with the `'full'` target (each car charged on arrival until full, never discharged), so the cars get the energy they need, without V2G, and the baseline is the same for every controller. The EV charging that the baseline adds back is the same whatever the controller does with the cars; the rest of the baseline keeps the controller's deferrable appliances and HVAC, so a controller that skips appliance cycles still lowers its baseline. On `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` over two weeks with `random_seed=0`, the baseline import goes from 4,068.3 to 7,714.0 kWh under both the BAU and `RBCSmartPolicy`; for `RBCSmartPolicy`, `district_energy_grid_ratio_to_baseline_import_total_ratio` goes from 1.535 to 0.809 and the legacy `cost_total` from 2.580 to 0.859. On `pt_rec_4` over one week, the baseline import goes from 243.2 to 358.9 kWh, against 350.2 kWh for the BAU run. On datasets with EV chargers every evaluation runs the BAU sidecar for this charging, also the legacy `evaluate()` and `evaluate_v2(include_business_as_usual=False)`, and it raises where the sidecar cannot run. Datasets without EV chargers and the simulation do not change, and this item does not change the BAU rows. `baseline_version=1` (constructor argument or schema key) keeps the 3.6.0 baseline; the version is recorded in `get_metadata()` and the export manifest, and `compare_exports()` reports runs under different versions as incompatible. See the [migration guide](migration.md#ev-charging-in-the-default-baseline) ([#365](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/365)).
- **District ratios to the baseline.** The district import, net-exchange and emissions ratios to the baseline were the unweighted mean of the building ratios, so small buildings with extreme ratios dominated them. They are now ratios of the district totals, like the cost, export and BAU ratios. The 3.6.0 values are kept as `*_building_mean_ratio`. ([#237](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/237))
- **District emissions.** District emissions summed each member's emissions while district import is netted, so local sharing never lowered emissions. They are now the emissions of the netted district import and match the provenance `external_purchase_emissions`. The sum over members is kept as `district_emissions_total_*_member_sum_kgco2`. ([#238](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/238))
- **Building demand-response settlement.** Building credited energy and shortfall were an uncapped contribution and an equal share of the request without the tolerance, so they did not add up to the district and buildings could report full compliance while the request failed. They are now the district values allocated with the revenue and penalty weights. District KPIs and cash flows are unchanged; the uncapped contribution is kept as a diagnostic. The definition version becomes `demand-response-audit-v2`. ([#240](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/240))
- **Load-factor penalties.** They were computed on the signed net series, so a window whose peak was not positive gave an infinite or negative penalty that was reported as a perfect 0. `evaluate_v2()` now uses import power and leaves windows without import undefined; the signed-net values are kept as `*_signed_net_*`, and the legacy `evaluate()` rows keep the signed series. ([#241](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/241))
- **Undefined service KPIs.** The deferrable start delay without completed cycles, ratios with a missing operand and each deferrable-appliance ratio to the BAU whose own two totals are zero (for example the missed-cycle ratio when neither run missed a cycle, even if cycles were due) are now missing instead of 0 or 1. ([#243](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/243))
- **Portfolio rates.** `MultiCommunityEnv` pools the portfolio EV departure rates and means, service levels, start delay and self-consumption from their additive denominators instead of weighting them by community. ([#244](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/244))
- **Portfolio market shares and demand-response compliance.** The portfolio community-market local shares of demand and export were means weighted by community `weight`. They are now pooled from local and grid energy. The portfolio demand-response compliance and audit coverage ratios are now pooled from requested energy. New district rows `district_demand_response_valid_requested_total_kwh` and `district_demand_response_credited_total_kwh` export the components of the compliance ratio; district and building values do not change. ([#285](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/285))
- **BAU ramping average.** `district_energy_grid_shape_quality_ramping_average_business_as_usual_kw` was the sum of upward ramps over the episode. It is now the mean per step transition; the sum is kept as `*_ramping_total_*_kw`. ([#245](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/245))
- **EV target met within 0.005 SOC.** A departure met its target only within 1e-6 SOC, while the rule-based baselines stop charging 0.001 below the target, so their cars counted as missed. The met test, the target feasibility and the departure-audit target now use a tolerance of 0.005 SOC, the constant `EV_SERVICE_SOC_TOLERANCE` in `citylearn.electric_vehicle`, capped by `ev_departure_service_tolerance`. The deficit and shortfall means stay exact. The departure audit's `definition_version` in the export metadata becomes `ev-departure-audit-v2`, so `compare_exports` reports exports from before and after this change as incompatible. This changes the met KPIs for every controller. On `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` over the full year (2,791 departures), `district_ev_performance_departure_success_ratio` goes from 0.988 to 0.994 for the BAU, 0.598 to 0.994 for `RBCBasicPolicy`, 0.495 to 0.994 for `RBCSmartPolicy` and 0.130 to 0.134 for uniform random actions; the feasible ratio goes from 0.606 to 1.000 (Basic) and 0.501 to 1.000 (Smart), and stays 1.000 for the BAU. On `pt_rec_15` over two weeks it goes from 0.4 to 1.0 (Basic) and 0.6 to 1.0 (Smart). The met counts quoted above for the episode-end departures (#370) and the EV SOC carry-over (#388) were measured with the exact test. With 0.005, on the same dataset with the BAU from step 1900, one run over steps 1900–1999 meets 33 of its 35 departures (30 before). Over two 100-step episodes, one run meets 70 of 72; the splits meet 67 under `shared` and 70 under `disjoint` without carry-over, and 70 under both with it. 30 × 24 splits meet 197 (`shared`) and 168 (`disjoint`) without carry-over, and 216 and 224 with it, the same as one run.
- **Comfort without set points.** A building whose dataset has no set-point columns has a 0 °C set point in the simulation, and the comfort KPIs judged it against it, so every occupied step was 21 °C too hot: on every `pt_rec_*` dataset each controller had a discomfort ratio of 1. The comfort KPIs of such a building (`comfort_resilience`, the `thermal_service_audit` discomfort rows and the legacy `discomfort_*` rows) are now undefined, and the district skips it. Physics and observations do not change. On `pt_rec_15` over two weeks, `district_thermal_service_audit_discomfort_ratio` goes from 1.0 to undefined for the BAU, both rule-based baselines and random actions, and the thermal coverage ratio from 1.0 to 0. Datasets with set points, such as Quebec, give the same values as before. The 2020–2022 challenge datasets and the three-phase demos have no set-point columns either, but their buildings have no occupants, so their comfort KPIs were already undefined and do not change.
- **Cost and emissions ratios of two zero totals.** `*_cost_ratio_to_*_total_ratio` and `*_emissions_ratio_to_*_total_ratio` were 1 when both totals were zero, for example the emissions ratios on Quebec, which has zero carbon intensity, and the cost ratios on the `citylearn_challenge_2020_climate_zone_*` and `citylearn_challenge_2021` datasets, which have no prices. They are now undefined at building, district and portfolio level, as the deferrable-appliance ratios already were. Other ratios keep 1 for two zero totals.
- **Total ratios with a negligible reference.** A building ratio to the baseline or the BAU became a meaningless large number when its reference total was close to zero but not zero: on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, Building_14's export ratio to the BAU was 3,961 (random actions) and Building_15's battery throughput ratio 2,443, and over the full year with zero actions Building_1's net-exchange ratio to the baseline was -720. A total ratio (`*_ratio_to_baseline_*_total_ratio`, `*_ratio_to_business_as_usual_*_ratio`) is now undefined when its reference is at most 1% of the mean |reference| of the building rows, the size of that reference for a typical member; the 0/0 rule is unchanged. On the bundled datasets only building rows change, all from finite to undefined: 8 of 8,774 rows on plus_evs (336 steps, RBCSmart or random), 3 over its full year with zero actions, 3 of 8,789 on `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo` (RBCSmart or random), none on `pt_rec_4`; no district row changes. Two of them were unremarkable values of a building with almost no reference (Building_15's PV generation ratio of 1.0 on 2 kWh, and its baseline export ratio of 0.90 under RBCSmart); the totals and deltas stay in the table. Control and reference totals that are equal within the zero tolerance keep the ratio 1.0, so a building the controller did not change no longer reads as undefined: with the BAU as controller on plus_evs, Building_14's export ratio to the BAU (0.2067 / 0.2067 kWh) and Building_15's battery throughput ratio (0.4295 / 0.4295 kWh) stay 1.0 ([#403](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/403)).

### Physics

Work package 4 of the 3.6.0 audit ([#226](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/226)), in [#279](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/279).

- **Versioned physics.** `physics_version` (schema key and `CityLearnEnv` argument) selects the physics of a run: `1` is the physics of 3.6.0 and earlier, and `2`, the default, holds the corrections of the physics work package of the 3.6.0 audit ([#226](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/226)). The BAU reference of `evaluate_v2()` uses the version of the evaluated environment, and the run metadata of the exports records it. See the [migration guide](migration.md#physics-version).
- **Standby loss of EVs away from their charger.** The away-from-charger SOC drift started from the previous step and discarded that step's standby loss, so an EV with a non-zero battery `loss_coefficient` kept its charge while away. Under physics version 2 the drift starts from the SOC after the loss, as it already did for suspended EVs ([#236](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/236)).
- **HVAC demand caps at steps longer than one hour.** The cooling and heating demand caps compared an energy per dataset step with the device's available power, which capped HVAC output at one hour of nominal power per step. Under physics version 2 the heat pump and the electric heater compare energy with energy ([#235](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/235)).
- **Autosizing at sub-hourly resolution.** Under physics version 2 storage tanks hold one hour of peak demand power (the `duration` autosize attribute changes it), so 15-minute data get the same tanks as hourly data instead of a quarter of them, and PV autosizing groups years by the dataset resolution and repeats the hourly PVWatts output at sub-hourly steps, where it used to fail ([#233](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/233)).
- **Battery discharge power.** `capacity_power_curve` describes how charging power tapers near full charge, but it also limited discharge, so a full battery could discharge at only a fraction of its nominal power. Under physics version 2 a new battery attribute, `discharge_power_curve`, limits discharge; it defaults to a flat curve at `nominal_power` and can derate at low state of charge, and `InventoryBattery` keeps its `max_discharge_kw` ([#227](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/227)).
- **Battery discharge during outages.** An islanded battery's discharge was capped by a pre-step load estimate that ignored the thermal storage discharge and the device limits, so the battery could discharge more than the building consumed and the surplus was reported as PV curtailment, even without PV. Under physics version 2 the battery discharges after the step's thermal demand and thermal storage discharges and only up to the islanded load that PV leaves, and `physics_invariant_checks` asserts it ([#229](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/229)).
- **Electrical-service headroom.** The import limit of a building with `electrical_service` was shared among the EV chargers and the battery in proportion to their requested power, including requests the device could not draw (a full battery, a charger without an EV), which curtailed the other devices. Under physics version 2 each request is first limited to the power its device can draw, and the headroom observations and the requested-violation penalty use the same powers ([#231](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/231)).
- **Thermal storage under an electrical-service limit.** The headroom was allocated before the step's thermal storage charging, so the import could exceed the limit while the recorded power, headroom and violation stayed within it. Under physics version 2 the headroom is allocated after the thermal, domestic hot water and non-shiftable updates, and the recorded power, headroom and violation come from the settled import ([#230](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/230)).
- **Deferrable appliance starts.** The start check used the realised future outage signal, load, PV and limits, which a controller cannot know, and reported the result through `can_start`, the action feedback and the refusal of the start. Under physics version 2 a start is decided on the current step and on whether the cycle ends within the episode; a cycle that later meets an outage is served from the islanded supply the other loads leave and, when that supply falls short, stops and is reported as missed ([#232](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/232)).
- **Energy to full of faded batteries.** `Battery.charge` stops at the degraded capacity, which capacity fade lowers below the nominal capacity, but the entity observations computed the energy a battery can still take from the nominal capacity. A battery that fade had made full was therefore observed as able to charge: over the year of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` with the BAU, chargers reported `can_charge` 1 and a positive `available_charge_power_kw` at 35,208 charger steps where the EV accepted nothing. The BAU reserved that power, and its home battery discharged for it and exported the energy. Under physics version 2, `energy_to_full_kwh`, `connected_ev_energy_to_full_kwh`, `flexible_energy_to_full_kwh` and the charge features derived from them stop at the lower of the SOC limit and the degraded capacity, for EV and stationary batteries. The simulated physics is unchanged, and batteries without fade, including every `pt_rec_*` battery, give the same observations. Without the `entity_core_electrical` bundle no observation shows the fade, so the BAU still estimates the EV headroom from the nominal capacity (see the [baselines guide](baselines.md#known-limits)).
- **One-way storage efficiency.** `StorageDevice.round_trip_efficiency` returned the one-way efficiency, the square root of `efficiency`. `one_way_efficiency` now holds that value, and `round_trip_efficiency` keeps it with a `DeprecationWarning`; the metadata and the entity feature `round_trip_efficiency_ratio` keep their names and values. `Battery.efficiency_history` gains one entry per charge or discharge instead of two ([#226](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/226)).
- **Frozen references for both physics versions.** The refactor parity audit (`scripts/audit/audit_refactor_parity.py`) and the NumPy version test (`tests/test_numpy_version_independence.py`) compared runs only with references recorded under physics version 1, so a change to the default physics passed both checks. Each check now runs under both versions and compares each run with the reference of its version: `scripts/audit/snapshots/refactor_parity_physics_v2.json` and `tests/data/numpy_version_reference_physics_v2.json` hold version 2, with the default `v2` random streams in the NumPy test, and the version 1 references are unchanged. The NumPy 1.x and 2.x CI jobs must both reproduce each reference. `--physics-version` selects one version in the audit ([#292](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/292)).

Physics version 1 reproduces 3.6.0: the BAU and `RBCSmartPolicy` give bit-identical series and `evaluate_v2()` tables to 3.6.0 on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, `citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation`, `pt_rec_4`, `pt_rec_30` and the outage week of `pt_rec_30/evaluation/rec_30_robustness.json`, and the frozen refactor and NumPy references run under it. Under version 2 the discharge curve changes the challenge datasets slightly: over four weeks of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` the BAU imports 0.05% less, and over the 30 days of `citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation` the BAU costs 0.25% more, because a full battery now discharges at its nominal power. The electrical-service and deferrable corrections leave cost, import and peak of the BAU and `RBCSmartPolicy` on `pt_rec_4` and `pt_rec_30` unchanged; they correct the recorded per-phase import peaks of `pt_rec_30` (from 19.3 to 17.4 kW on L1 for the BAU, for example), which overstated the settled import, and in the outage week of `rec_30_robustness.json` the BAU's unserved deferrable energy falls from 1.47 to 0.28 kWh, because cycles are no longer refused for outages the controller could not know of. The energy-to-full correction changes the BAU on datasets whose EV batteries fade and that publish the `entity_core_electrical` bundle: over the year of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, the BAU's home batteries export 51 kWh less of what they discharged for full EVs, member import falls by 46 kWh and the cost by 0.03% (32,211.91 to 32,202.41 EUR), and district import rises by 0.03% because that export no longer offsets other members' import; the EV departure KPIs are unchanged. Over one day of the 15-second three-phase demos, that battery export falls from 3.10 kWh to below 0.01 kWh and the BAU cost rises by 0.08 to 0.09%. `pt_rec_*` and `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo` are unchanged. Rerun results under version 2, or set `physics_version=1` to reproduce 3.6.0.

### Random streams and seeds

Work package 5 of the 3.6.0 audit ([#226](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/226)), in [#281](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/281). Results change for runs that use random device parameters, electric vehicles away from their charger, occupants or stochastic outages; `random_stream_version='v1'` restores the 3.6.0 random streams.

- **Random streams are derived per purpose, object and episode.** Random device parameters, the state of charge of electric vehicles away from their charger, occupant set-point interactions and stochastic outages reused or shared their random streams ([#248](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/248)). Every unspecified device parameter was the first draw of a new `RandomState`, so all of them sat at the same quantile; the EV drift seed was `random_seed + episode`, so seed `s` in episode 1 repeated seed `s + 1` in episode 0, and one stream was shared by every EV; every occupant drew `RandomState(max(random_seed, 1) + time_step)`, the same value in every building and episode; and `ReliabilityMetricsPowerOutage` skipped day 0 and the partial last day and floored durations. A new `random_stream_version` setting (schema key and constructor argument) selects the derivation. The default, `v2`, derives one `numpy.random.SeedSequence` stream per purpose, object and episode, keeps one generator per stream, and draws outages on every day with durations rounded to the nearest step. `v1` reproduces 3.6.0 for the same seed. On `citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation` the three buildings no longer share identical outages in every episode, and on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` the 168-hour net consumption changes by −1.6%. Runs without these random features are unchanged. See the [migration guide](migration.md#random-streams).
- **Agent and neighborhood-generation streams.** `TabularQLearning` drew its exploration decision, and MARLISA with information sharing shuffled its coordination order, with `RandomState(random_seed + time_step)`, and the neighborhood generator seeded partial-load simulation `i` of building `b` with `b + i`, so their streams overlapped across seeds, time steps and buildings ([#287](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/287)). With `v2`, agents keep one `SeedSequence` generator per purpose and episode and follow the `random_stream_version` of their environment, and `Neighborhood` takes a `random_stream_version` argument that derives the seed of each partial-load simulation from the neighborhood seed, the building and the simulation index. Agent exploration and newly generated datasets change; simulations and the bundled datasets do not, and `v1` restores the 3.6.0 streams.
- **`TabularQLearning` seeds its action spaces.** Random exploration actions, and the fallback action for an observation whose Q-values are all still NaN, were sampled from the gymnasium action spaces of `TabularQLearningWrapper`, which nothing seeded, so gymnasium seeded them from OS entropy and two runs with the same `random_seed` did not repeat. The agent now seeds each action space that is not already seeded from `random_seed`, as `SAC` does, and a space the caller has seeded keeps its stream. This applies with both `random_stream_version` values: these actions were never reproducible, so `v1` has no stream to preserve.
- **What `reset(seed=s)` repeats.** The migration guide, the `CityLearnEnv.reset` docstring and `EpisodeTracker.random_stream_episode` said that `reset(seed=s)` repeats the first episode of an environment built with `random_seed=s`. It restarts the per-episode streams (EV drift, occupant interactions, stochastic outages) at the key of episode 0, but the episode window still advances, and device and EV battery parameters and the initial EV state of charge keep the values drawn at construction. The documentation now says so, and recommends building a new environment to repeat a run with another seed and passing a seed to the first reset only, since a seed at every reset repeats the same streams in every episode. The behaviour is unchanged: making a seeded reset rebuild the environment or rewind the window would change every run that seeds its resets ([#303](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/303)).
- **Cloned members draw their own streams.** Under a dynamic topology, an `add_member` event that clones a member from `source_member_id` renamed the copy but kept the source's random stream keys, so the clone's occupant drew exactly the same set-point interactions as its source in every step and episode, and any later draw from its devices repeated the source's. The clone and everything under it (occupant, devices, chargers, appliances) are now keyed by the new member's name. Results change only for schemas that clone members with occupants or other random draws, under `random_stream_version='v2'`; `v2` is not yet released, `v1` is unchanged, and no bundled dataset clones a member. Device parameters drawn at construction are still copied from the source ([#304](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/304)).

### Engineering

- The test suite copies bundled datasets into a temporary cache private to each pytest process instead of the user's cache (`~/.cache/citylearn`), which overwrote downloaded datasets and made concurrent test runs fail with `FileNotFoundError` ([#256](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/256)).
- New tests: heat-pump COP (formula and the cap at 20, including a zero temperature difference), the private test cache, the optional end-use load-profile modules (when their extras are installed) and the operator balance.
- **Property-based energy conservation.** `tests/test_property_energy_conservation.py` uses Hypothesis to draw the step length (60 s to 1 h), the physics and settlement versions, outages, phase limits, member tariffs, member and asset exit and return, and every building, charger and washer action of two-member community episodes, plus random flat actions on the 15-minute EV demo at its own and shorter steps. Every run must pass the physics invariant checks and close the independent energy, cash and carbon balances of `IndependentBalanceAudit`. The search is derandomized (80 examples, about 8 s), and `hypothesis` joins `test_requirements.txt` ([#289](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/289)).
- The publish workflow can use PyPI trusted publishing (OpenID Connect) once a trusted publisher is configured and the repository variable `PYPI_TRUSTED_PUBLISHING` is `true`; until then it uses the existing credentials. The pip cache key of the test workflow hashes `pyproject.toml` instead of the removed `setup.py`. The stale `README_PYPI.md` is removed ([#256](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/256)).
- Documentation: `settlement_version`, `grid_export_price`, `tariff_files` and `local_trading_enabled` are documented in the schema reference; the community import-share formula, the PV self-consumption formula (curtailment term) and the timing of the entity PV features are corrected; the `BasicElectricVehicleRBC_ReferenceController` and `RLC` docstrings describe what the code does ([#256](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/256)).
- `scripts/audit/audit_performance_results.py` no longer compares long RBC and SAC runs with `scripts/audit/snapshots/rbc_kpi_baseline.json` and `sac_kpi_baseline.json`. Both were written at 3.0.0 from another checkout, no check read them, and the KPI definitions have changed since. The files and the `--update-baselines`, `--kpi-tol`, `--episode-steps`, `--snapshot-dir` and `--strict-require-sac-snapshot` options are removed; `run_full_audit.py` drops `--episode-steps`. The script keeps its step-time and invariant-overhead checks, which now run with the same `time_step_ratio_mode="compress"` as `scripts/ci/perf_smoke.py`. Results are checked by the refactor parity audit and the frozen references of the test suite ([#314](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/314)).

- **Links and versions.** Dead links are fixed, a weekly job checks external links, and every page says that the site documents `master` and where to read the documentation of a release ([#270](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/270), [#271](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/271)).
- **Notebook outputs.** The notebook pages (`usage/agents`, `usage/load_environment`, `usage/cli`, `usage/rl_training`) show the outputs of a CI run: the Sphinx workflow executes the notebooks with `scripts/ci/run_notebooks.py --output-dir examples` before the build, and `check_docs.py --require-notebook-outputs` fails when a page has none. The notebooks in the repository stay free of outputs ([#299](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/299)).

### Installation, documentation and CLI

Work package 9 of the 3.6.0 audit ([#226](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/226)), part B. Simulations and KPIs are unchanged.

- **PyTorch and scikit-learn are optional.** A default installation took about 5.7 GB, mostly PyTorch with its CUDA libraries, although only LSTM thermal dynamics and the SAC and MARLISA controllers use it ([#259](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/259)). `pip install citylearn` now installs about 200 MB. The new extras `citylearn[dynamics]` (PyTorch, for the LSTM thermal dynamics of the 2023 challenge, Quebec, `baeda_3dem` and EULP neighbourhood datasets) and `citylearn[rl]` (PyTorch and scikit-learn, for SAC and MARLISA) add them, and `end_use_load_profiles` includes both. Loading such a dataset or controller without them raises an `ImportError` that names the extra and the CPU-only PyTorch command. The installation page starts with the installation steps, lists the extras and documents the dataset cache, offline use and every `CITYLEARN_*` variable; the reproducibility notes and the compatibility table move to its end. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-360-to-370).
- **Actionable data errors.** An unknown dataset name suggests the closest names and points to `DataSet().get_dataset_names()`, not the non-existent `get_names()`. A Parquet dataset without pyarrow fails before the download and names `citylearn[parquet]`. Offline errors with an empty cache name the cache folder, `CITYLEARN_LOCAL_DATASETS_PATH` and how to disable offline mode, and the log messages say "does not exist" instead of "DNE" ([#268](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/268)).
- **Example notebooks run in CI.** The controller notebook failed in its passive-agent cell, whose environment no longer matches the native BAU reference; it now evaluates without that reference ([#265](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/265)). The notebooks simulate one week, install `citylearn[rl]` and Stable Baselines3 2.4 or newer instead of the pin 2.3.2, which downgraded gymnasium to 0.29.1, and no longer run RLlib 2.10, which has no wheels for Python 3.12 or newer ([#266](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/266)). A new CI job executes every published notebook. A new tutorial, [Train and evaluate a reinforcement learning controller](https://www.citylearn.net/usage/rl_training.html), trains a Stable Baselines3 SAC controller, evaluates it on an unseen week and compares it with the BAU on a scorecard; the 2.1.2 tutorial notebook is removed ([#267](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/267)).
- **CLI.** `train --evaluate` evaluates on the first training episode window instead of the whole dataset, so `-ke '{"episode_time_steps": 24}'` evaluates 24 steps. The training summary records the host and user names only with `--record_host`. `simulate` prints the path of each file it writes, and a Stable Baselines3 agent without a policy raises a `TypeError` that shows `-ka '{"policy": "MlpPolicy"}'`. Evaluating a wrapped environment (for example a Stable Baselines3 agent) failed with `AttributeError` on gymnasium 1.x and now works. The documentation states the actual requirements and gives a Stable Baselines3 example ([#269](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/269)).
- **Contributing.** `CONTRIBUTING.md` covers the setup, a fast test subset, the documentation and notebook checks and the pull request checklist; the issue templates are assigned to the current maintainer, a question template is added, and `ray[rllib]` is no longer a test dependency ([#273](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/273)).

## v3.6.0 - 2026-11-19

### What's new

3.6.0 collects 26 pull requests: corrections to the physics, KPIs, baselines, agents, data loading and exports, faster simulation steps, three opt-in features, and a Python 3.10 floor with a wider CI. Results change only where a fix applies; the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360) lists which runs to repeat.

### Fixes

Physics and KPIs:

- **Power and energy at sub-hourly steps.** Several limits compared a power in kW with an energy per step in kWh, which only coincide at 3600 s. `available_nominal_power` is now a power at every step length; heat pumps and electric heaters no longer exceed `nominal_power` when they charge thermal storage; on native sub-hourly data a device delivers at most `nominal_power` x step duration per step; during an outage, the local supply passed to devices and to the non-shiftable load is converted to power and to dataset resolution; and the `cooling_demand`, `heating_demand` and `dhw_demand` observations convert the storage discharge to dataset resolution when `time_step_ratio` is not 1. ([#185](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/185))
- **Undersized devices.** A device that cannot meet its demand no longer raises `AssertionError: demand is greater than ..._device max output`. Its output is clipped, a `RuntimeWarning` is issued once per device and episode, and the shortfall is reported as unserved thermal service energy. ([#185](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/185))
- **Base-power estimate.** The estimate behind electrical-service headroom and the feasibility KPIs no longer subtracts thermal storage discharge twice, and it counts escalator power, including a requested escalator state in the step it is requested. ([#185](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/185))
- **Clip reasons.** `clip_reason_phase_headroom` and `clip_reason_export_headroom` name only the asset's own phases and the clipped direction. ([#185](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/185))
- **Normalized observations.** `observations(normalize=True, periodic_normalization=False)` no longer raises `KeyError: 'month'`. ([#185](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/185))
- **Occupant set-point hold.** `set_point_hold_time_steps` in a building schema now reaches `LogisticRegressionOccupantInteractionBuilding`. On the Quebec neighbourhood datasets (4 steps), occupant set-point changes revert to the schedule after 4 steps without interaction instead of holding for the rest of the episode. ([#185](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/185))
- **Dynamic topology district baseline.** The district reference series summed only the members active at evaluation time, while the control series keep every member's past consumption, so a departed member counted on the control side only. With all-zero actions on `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_topology_demo` (24 steps, `Building_6` leaving at step 12), the district import delta to baseline was 12.76 kWh, the export delta -11.00 kWh and the emissions delta 4.44 kg, although control and baseline coincide; they are now below 4e-6, the float32 rounding of the reference series. The reference series now sum every member that has been active, each over the steps at which it was a member. ([#182](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/182))
- **Electrical-service histories across topology events.** A member's requested-pressure, total-power and per-phase power histories were erased when it rejoined or when one of its assets changed, and the electrical-service and phase KPIs lost the earlier steps. They are now kept. ([#182](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/182))
- **Robustness noise.** With `noise_std > 0`, Gaussian noise could make solar generation, solar irradiance and carbon intensity negative, add generation at night and push outdoor relative humidity outside [0, 100] %. One rule now bounds the noisy values: solar quantities stay `>= 0` and get no noise at night, humidity stays within [0, 100] % and carbon intensity stays `>= 0`. The noise draws are unchanged, so values within range are bit-identical. Solar generation noise uses the generation series for its shape instead of the indoor temperature series; both have the same length, so the draws are the same. Runs with `noise_std = 0`, including all bundled datasets, are unchanged. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360). ([#204](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/204))
- **Battery discharge features during outages.** During a power outage the entity `storage` table reported `can_discharge = 0` and `available_discharge_power_kw = 0`, although the battery can discharge into the islanded building up to its local load ([#220](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/220)). These features now report the battery's discharge capacity without the grid export headroom, as #205 does for vehicle-to-building discharge; the simulator still limits the discharge to the local load. The rule-based baselines read `can_discharge`, so they now use the battery during outages. In the week with the most outages of `pt_rec_30/evaluation/rec_30_robustness.json` and `rec_30_combined.json`, the BAU discharges 1.2 kWh more and the normalized unserved energy during outages falls from 0.0015 to 0.0007 and from 0.0017 to 0.0008; cost and emissions are unchanged. In `pt_rec_100/evaluation/rec_100_allin.json` the observations change but the BAU KPIs of that week do not. Datasets without outages, and the flat interface, are unchanged. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360). This closes [#220](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/220). ([#221](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/221))

Baselines:

- **Baselines without the `entity_core_electrical` bundle.** Two changes apply only to datasets whose schema does not enable that bundle; `pt_rec_*` and `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` enable it and are unchanged. First, the BAU battery reserved the charger's nominal power for a connected EV even when the EV accepted much less, for example in the last step before 100% SOC, and discharged for that demand; it now reserves the power the EV can accept, estimated from the observed SOC, battery capacity, charger power and efficiency as the simulator computes `available_charge_power_kw` ([#190](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/190)). Second, every baseline took the last interval's export as PV surplus, so when a battery discharge overshot the load and was exported, the battery recharged from the grid in the next step (22.8 kWh in the first 48 hours of `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo` with the BAU); only PV above the observed load now counts as surplus. The definition versions become `immediate-full-charge-v3`, `immediate-user-target-v2`, `basic-v3`, `smart-local-v3` and `community-v3`. Over 30 days, the control cost changes by −0.2% to −2.4% and the emissions by −0.8% to −2.5% for every baseline on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all`, `citylearn_challenge_2023_phase_2_local_evaluation` and `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo`, except the default BAU on the last one, which costs 0.4% more because its battery now stores PV that previously went to the community market. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360). The first change addresses consequence 2 of [#190](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/190); consequence 1, the one-step battery lag, is unchanged, and [#190](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/190) stays open. ([#213](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/213))
- **Baselines read corrupted observations under the flat interface.** Under the flat interface the env corrupts only the flat vectors, and the baselines, which read entity observations, decided from uncorrupted values; under the entity interface they read the corrupted ones ([#192](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/192)). Run as controllers (`predict(observations)`), they now get the step's robustness observation events applied to their entity observations as the entity interface applies them, and take the same actions under both interfaces. No random stream is consumed, and the env's flat observations and robustness history are unchanged; the controller KPIs of flat runs with observation events can change. The native BAU reference of `evaluate_v2()` (`predict([])`) stays uncorrupted. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360). ([#212](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/212))

Agents and wrappers ([#208](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/208)):

- **`TabularQLearning`** no longer bootstraps from the next state on terminal transitions.
- **`MARLISARBC`** explores with its RBC; it used to explore with random actions.
- **`MARLISA` with `pca_compression < 1`** sizes its networks to the PCA output instead of failing at the first update.
- **Discrete wrappers.** `TabularQLearningObservationWrapper` declares exactly the `prod(bins)` states it encodes (it declared `prod(bins + 1) - 1` and could encode a state outside the space), and `DiscreteObservationWrapper` keeps values above the observation space in the top bin. `DiscreteObservationWrapper` and `DiscreteActionWrapper` bin the spaces of the env they wrap, so they work over the normalized wrappers. `ClippedObservationWrapper` no longer modifies the environment's cached observations.
- **`OnehotEncoding`** raises `ValueError` for a value outside its classes.

Data loading ([#209](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/209)):

- **Charger files with blank or float states.** Charger states are parsed as numbers. A blank cell used to make every state of the charger unreadable, so no EV connected.
- **`InventoryBattery` EVs** load without `nominal_power`, which the battery derives from `max_charge_kw` and `max_discharge_kw`.
- **Random initial EV SOC** is drawn within the battery's declared `min_soc` and `max_soc`; an `InventoryBattery` used to fail when the draw from [0, 1] fell outside them.
- **Entity observations with `max_soc < 1`** report `soc_max_ratio`, the energy to full and the available charge power up to `max_soc` instead of a full battery.

Datasets and dataset cache:

- **Incomplete dataset caches.** A cached dataset counted as complete as soon as its `schema.json` existed, so a cache left by an interrupted download raised `FileNotFoundError` for its first missing file. A cache is now complete only when every input file that `CityLearnEnv` reads from the schema exists; an incomplete cache is replaced in one step, and offline mode names the missing files ([#176](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/176), [#181](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/181)).
- **Dataset environment variables.** `CITYLEARN_OFFLINE`, `CITYLEARN_DATASET_GITHUB_ACCOUNT`, `CITYLEARN_DATASET_REPOSITORY` and `CITYLEARN_DATASET_TAG` are read when `CityLearnEnv` or `DataSet` is created instead of once at import, and `CityLearnEnv(offline=None)` honours `CITYLEARN_OFFLINE` ([#198](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/198)).
- **Dataset names.** `DataSet.get_dataset_names()` keeps a dataset whose name occurs in its parent path and accepts a `datasets_path` with a trailing slash; concurrent first calls list GitHub once and write the cached list atomically. The misc files share the dataset cache code ([#199](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/199)).
- **15 s Parquet datasets load from the dataset cache.** The schemas of `citylearn_three_phase_electrical_service_demo_15s_parquet`, `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_asset_changes_demo_15s_parquet` and `citylearn_three_phase_dynamic_assets_only_demo_15s_parquet` set a repository-relative `root_directory`. Passing the schema path returned by `DataSet().get_dataset(name)` to `CityLearnEnv` resolved it against the cache folder and raised `FileNotFoundError` for `<cache>/<dataset>/data/datasets/<dataset>/Building_1.parquet` ([#194](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/194)). The three schemas now set `"root_directory": null`, like the other datasets. `CityLearnEnv(name)` and schema paths inside a repository checkout already found the files and still read the same folder. ([#197](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/197))

Exports, CLI and files ([#207](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/207), [#202](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/202), [#203](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/203)):

- **BAU time-series export.** The BAU export writes the executed time steps only, like the control exports. It used to add the terminal step, a copy of the last executed step, which inflated totals summed from the file.
- **Charger exports.** Without an EV, the charger row writes `-1.00` under `EV SOC-%` instead of adding an `EV SOC` column.
- **CLI evaluation.** `citylearn simulate ... evaluate` and `train --evaluate` no longer fail (`All arrays must be of the same length`, or `not enough values to unpack` with `-te`), and `train --evaluate` evaluates the trained agent instead of a new one.
- **Schema agent attributes.** `load_agent()` without `agent` keeps `schema['agent']['attributes']` when keyword arguments such as `env=` are passed; keyword arguments override them. `Simulator(..., agent_name=None)` therefore builds the schema's agent with the schema's parameters.
- **Windows.** `run_work_order()` activates virtual environments with `activate.bat`, which `cmd.exe` can run. JSON and YAML files are read and written as UTF-8, so non-ASCII text in schemas is no longer decoded as cp1252.
- **Multi-community KPI comparison.** `load_kpis()` and `compare_exports()` read multi-community KPI exports, which repeat each name once per community, instead of raising `Duplicate KPI/name rows`.
- **JSON files with NumPy values.** `FileHandler.write_json` writes NumPy booleans, integers and floats as JSON booleans and numbers, and NumPy arrays of booleans and numbers as lists, instead of their text (`2.5` instead of `"2.5"`, `[1.0, null]` instead of `"[ 1. nan]"`). With `ignore_nan=False`, a NaN NumPy float raises `ValueError` like a Python float. namedtuples and `Decimal` values are written as before, and the export manifests are unchanged. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360). ([#203](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/203))
- **pandas 3.** Exports no longer emit the two pandas 3 deprecation warnings, and a Parquet render column that mixes text and numbers is written instead of raising `ArrowTypeError`. Export files are unchanged ([#202](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/202)).

### Performance

None of these changes a result; each PR compared trajectories and KPIs bit for bit with master.

- **Single time steps.** Per-step code reads one time step of device and storage series instead of rebuilding the whole series, and `evaluate_v2()` builds its records without DataFrame scans ([#183](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/183)).
- **Time-series windows.** `TimeSeriesData` keeps each variable's episode window as a plain attribute, so reading weather, pricing, carbon, charger and energy-simulation values no longer goes through a Python `__getattribute__` and a new slice per read ([#210](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/210)).
- **District counterfactuals.** The twelve district `*_without_storage*` series sum a NumPy matrix instead of building a DataFrame; this speeds up `evaluate()` and `evaluate_v2()`, not the simulation step ([#206](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/206)).
- **Baseline resets.** The baselines build their policy engine once per agent instead of at every episode reset; the first `predict()` after a reset takes 48 ms instead of 81 ms (flat) and 22 ms instead of 54 ms (entity) on `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs` ([#211](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/211)).

### New opt-in features

These are off by default; existing schemas and results are unchanged.

- **Reward for load left unserved during power outages.** During an outage a building cannot import, so its net consumption is zero and rewards built on grid import give no signal about the load that PV and storage leave unserved. `OutageUnservedEnergyReward(coefficient, base_reward)` subtracts `coefficient` times that unserved energy from a base reward, and the new flat observation `power_outage_unserved_energy` (kWh per step, last settled step) gives it per building with the definition of the `power_outage_normalized_unserved_energy_total` KPI ([#188](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/188)). The reward works under both interfaces; the observation is flat only. Both are opt-in, so existing schemas and results are unchanged. ([#195](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/195))
- **Time-varying available grid power.** `buildings.<name>.electrical_service.available_grid_power` references a per-step series of the power a building may import, in kW, from a file with one column per building or a named column ([#119](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/119)). The import limit in force at a step is the smaller of the static `limits.total.import_kw` and the series value, and every reader of the total import limit uses it: the electrical-service allocation (EV chargers and the battery are curtailed first; fixed load stays served and counts as a violation), deferrable start feasibility, the headroom observations, the EV feasibility KPI and the electrical-service violation KPI. A limit of 0 kW does not island the building; outages stay in `power_outage`. The new flat observation `available_grid_power` reports the limit for the coming interval when a series is set, and the rule-based baselines cap their planned import headroom with it. Buildings without a series are unchanged. See the [schema reference](schema_reference.md#available-grid-power). ([#216](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/216))
- **Vehicle-to-building backup during outages (opt-in).** With `power_outage.vehicle_to_building: true`, a connected EV may discharge to its building while the grid is down, as a vehicle-to-home backup. The discharge is limited to the non-shiftable load and the thermal device load that PV and the battery leave uncovered, so the building exports nothing to the failed grid. Under the entity interface, the charger's `can_discharge` and `available_discharge_power_kw` report the vehicle's discharge capacity during the outage. The discharge can take the vehicle below its required departure SOC; that is left to the controller and shows in the EV departure KPIs. The option is off by default, and results without it are unchanged. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360). ([#205](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/205))

### Build and CI

- **Python 3.10 or newer.** CityLearn now requires Python 3.10 and is tested on Python 3.10 to 3.14, with NumPy 1.x on 3.10. Python 3.9 reached its end of life in October 2025. On Python 3.9, `pip` keeps resolving CityLearn 3.5.0. The CLI commands `simulate train` and `simulate evaluate` used `datetime.UTC`, which exists only from Python 3.11, and failed on Python 3.10; they now use `datetime.timezone.utc`. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-350-to-360). ([#218](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/218))
- **Package metadata in `pyproject.toml`.** The metadata moves from `setup.py` to a PEP 621 `[project]` table and `setup.py` is removed; the wheel holds the same files ([#201](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/201)).
- **Install smoke and compatibility table.** CI builds the wheel, installs it with its PyPI dependencies and runs a simulation and export smoke on macOS (arm64) and Windows with Python 3.10 and 3.14; the Ubuntu build job already ran it on the installed wheel ([#138](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/138)). The installation guide gains a compatibility table of Python and dependency versions for every release from 1.5.0 ([#150](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/issues/150)), kept in step with `requirements.txt` by a test, and the GitHub Actions are updated ([#217](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/217)).
- **Windows paths in tests.** The nested-dataset download tests compare paths in POSIX form, so they pass on Windows ([#200](https://github.com/citylearn-project/CityLearn/pull/200)).

### What's new

- **Portugal REC datasets.** Four synthetic Portuguese renewable energy communities with 4, 15, 30 and 100 residential and commercial members, 2023-2024 at 15 minutes: `pt_rec_4`, `pt_rec_15`, `pt_rec_30` and `pt_rec_100`. - **Nested dataset folders.** `DataSet.get_dataset()` now downloads a dataset's subfolders; it previously fetched only the top-level files. A download costs two GitHub API requests however deeply the dataset is nested, and the files come from raw.githubusercontent.com. Set `CITYLEARN_GITHUB_TOKEN` or `GITHUB_TOKEN` to raise the anonymous limit of 60 API requests per hour. The contents listing now uses the account and repository given to `DataSet`.

No dataset, schema, export or BAU migration is required; existing datasets and KPIs are unchanged. See the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-340-to-350).

### Validation

## v3.4.0 - 2026-11-16

### What's new

- **Two selectable BAUs.** `BusinessAsUsualAgent` charges a connected EV at once and at full power, to 100% by default (`immediate-full-charge-v2`). With `ev_charge_target='requested'` (`immediate-user-target-v1`) it charges only up to the user's requested departure SOC; the last command is limited to the remaining energy gap, and below the charger minimum the EV stops slightly short of the target instead of overshooting it. `business_as_usual_ev_charge_target` (constructor or schema) selects which one `evaluate_v2()` uses as the KPI reference. The previous Basic, Smart and Community controllers and the names `NormalPolicy`, `NormalNoBatteryPolicy`, `ServiceOnlyBaselineAgent`, `GridAwareBaselineAgent` and `CommunityAwareBaselineAgent` are removed.
- **Basic, Smart and Community baselines.** `RBCBasicPolicy`, `RBCSmartPolicy` and `RBCCommunityPolicy` are replaced by the baselines validated in the Soft-CPS Algorithms repository. Basic uses the current price and service urgency. Smart schedules EVs and appliances from price and local PV-surplus forecasts and combines battery self-consumption, arbitrage and reserve. Community adds shareable member energy and, with the community market enabled, battery support during expensive periods once community import reaches 15 kW.
- **Unlimited headroom is no longer 0 kW.** In the entity tables, a building without an electrical service limit publishes its headroom as 1,000,000 kW (the observation bound) instead of 0 kW, which read as "no capacity left" and stopped rule-based EV charging.
- **One observation contract.** All baselines decide from the named entity observations and run under the flat and the entity interface, including dynamic topology. Keyword arguments set any policy parameter; unknown names raise an error. Experiment manifests record the definition version and resolved parameters.

BAU-relative KPIs change with the new reference. Rerun the controller and its reference together; see the [migration guide](migration.md#upgrade-from-33-to-340) and the [baselines guide](baselines.md).

### Validation

On the same observations, the CityLearn baselines request exactly the same actions as the Algorithms policies (commit `725a72a`) at every step, for both BAUs, Basic, Smart and Community, on three bundled datasets (EVs, three-phase limits with a community market, dynamic topology).

On 30 days of `citylearn_challenge_2022_phase_all_plus_evs`, the default BAU delivers the same EV energy as the 3.3.1 BAU (7,512 kWh) and costs 4.8% more (2,723 € vs 2,598 €): its battery reacts to the last observed step instead of reading the current step from the simulator. The user-target BAU costs 2,290 € and delivers 4,797 kWh. Basic, Smart and Community meet the minimum acceptable SOC at every feasible departure.
